# CARE International — Vietnam flood

## Summary
CARE International's Vietnam flood work sits inside "Forecast-based Early Action" (FBEA),
an EU Humanitarian Aid (ECHO)-funded consortium programme run jointly with Plan
International and World Vision, alongside Vietnamese government and Red Cross partners.
It is not a CARE-only framework: the Hub attributes the "coordinating" role to CARE, but
implementation is shared across the three NGOs and rotates by year and site. The programme
has run in at least two phases — an initial pilot (2021-2022) in Quang Tri and Binh Thuan
provinces, and a follow-on "FBEA-SEA" phase (from 2024) that widened scope to drought and
heatwave and added Ho Chi Minh City — building forecast-based trigger thresholds, SOPs and
community early-action capacity rather than operating a mature automatic-disbursement
mechanism.

## Trigger
The only published quantitative threshold found is from a 2023 journal article
(Vietnam Institute of Meteorology, Hydrology and Climate Change-affiliated) describing
trigger-development work for Thanh and Thuan communes, Huong Hoa district, Quang Tri: a
maximum 1-day forecast rainfall of ≥150mm is proposed to trigger early action for a
"medium flood," developed using the German Red Cross's forecast-based-financing
threshold-building method and back-tested against 2022 forecast data with "relatively
consistent" results. No lead time is stated, no equivalent threshold was found for the
Binh Thuan sites, and public sources describe this as project technical/pilot work rather
than a confirmed, funded, automatically-triggering mechanism.

## Funding & scope
EU Humanitarian Aid (ECHO) funds the consortium. The Anticipation Hub's Vietnam country
page attributes $168,415 in pre-arranged funding and 4,190 targeted people to this
CARE-coordinated flood record specifically. Other project sources give different, harder
to reconcile figures for overlapping scopes: World Vision's own FBEA phase-1 (Jul
2021-Dec 2022) component was budgeted at EUR 143,000 (~USD 173,030) for 4,560 direct
beneficiaries in Ham Thuan Bac district, Binh Thuan; the follow-on FBEA-SEA phase (18
months from a Sep 2024 page date) states a consortium-wide minimum of 6,578 direct
beneficiaries across Quang Tri, Binh Thuan and Ho Chi Minh City, covering flood, drought
and heatwave. See `extra.funding_note`.

## Activations
None known. No source describes a real (trigger-fired, funds-disbursed) activation for
either FBEA phase; the Hub's listed years (2023, 2024) correspond to consortium project
activity — trigger design, testing, community capacity-building — rather than a
documented early-action release.
