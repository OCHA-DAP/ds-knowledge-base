# Oxfam — Philippines drought

## Summary
Oxfam Pilipinas leads SUPREME BARMM, an EU Civil Protection and Humanitarian Aid
(ECHO)-funded consortium working with the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim
Mindanao (BARMM) regional government to run anticipatory action for El Niño-driven
drought. Built with BARMM READi and the region's Pre-Disaster Risk Assessment (PDRA)
Group, it monitors PAGASA El Niño alerts and Manila Observatory drought-risk analysis
across BARMM's Special Geographic Areas. Its first drought activation, during the
2023-24 El Niño, delivered water storage containers, hygiene kits and drought-resistant
vegetable seeds to roughly 2,000 families (~5,665 people) — reported as the Philippines'
first anticipatory action for drought.

## Trigger
The protocol is tiered ("level 1" and beyond), escalating with PAGASA's El Niño
Alert/Advisory probability — e.g. rising from 40% in April to 80% in May 2023 — and
Manila Observatory analysis identifying BARMM's Special Geographic Areas as high-risk
for El Niño impact (BARMM already assessed at "medium risk of drought"). A level 1
activation triggers household awareness-raising sessions and distribution of rainwater
harvesters/water containers; the 2023-24 activation additionally distributed hygiene
kits and drought-resistant vegetable seeds once observable impacts (falling well-water
supply, visible farmland damage) appeared. Assistance was distributed by February 2024,
several months after the initial May 2023 alert. No published document gives exact
quantitative thresholds (e.g. an SPI value or rainfall percentile) for this specific
protocol; a 2020 FAO drought-AA model for Mindanao — built on a weighted 10-variable
forecast index with monitoring/assessment/action thresholds at 33%/50% — is the closest
public reference for the region's drought-trigger methodology, but it is not confirmed
to be the exact formula Oxfam's BARMM protocol uses.

## Funding & scope
Funded by the EU's Civil Protection and Humanitarian Aid department (ECHO) through the
SUPREME BARMM consortium; Oxfam's own reporting does not disclose the specific
disbursed amount. The Anticipation Hub's inventory records US$68,281 pre-arranged and
5,665 people targeted for this framework — consistent in scale with the ~2,000
households reported in press coverage. Geographic scope is BARMM's Special Geographic
Areas (SGAs); specific municipalities are not detailed in the sources found. Named
implementing partners for the drought framework specifically are Oxfam, the People's
Disaster Risk Reduction Network, and Community Organizers Multiversity (per the
Anticipation Hub record); the broader multi-hazard SUPREME BARMM consortium includes
additional NGOs and Manila Observatory.

## Activations
- **~February 2024** (2023-24 El Niño) — the Philippines' first drought AA activation.
  Following PAGASA's El Niño Alert (hoisted 2 May 2023) and a BARMM Office of Civil
  Defense risk-assessment meeting, SUPREME BARMM distributed water storage containers,
  hygiene kits and drought-resistant vegetable seeds to ~2,000 families (~5,665 people)
  in BARMM's Special Geographic Areas ahead of anticipated El Niño impacts (falling
  well-water levels, farmland damage). Reported by Oxfam Pilipinas and SunStar
  Davao/Zamboanga.
- No prior activations known; Oxfam Pilipinas reports 12 AA activations across all
  hazards in the Philippines since 2019, of which this is the drought-specific one
  captured here.
