# Oxfam — Guatemala drought

## Summary
Oxfam's Guatemala drought entry on the Anticipation Hub inventory corresponds to its role as a
CERF-funded sub-implementing partner — through local partner ASEDECHI (Asociación de Servicios
y Desarrollo Socioeconómico de Chiquimula) — delivering the nutrition-response component of the
OCHA/CERF collective Dry Corridor drought anticipatory action framework for Guatemala. Oxfam
does not run an independent trigger or publish its own EAP for this hazard; it activates when
the collective framework (ECMWF SEAS5-triggered, led by FAO/PAHO/UNICEF/WFP with OCHA and the
Government of Guatemala) reaches its pre-agreed threshold, delivering integrated health-and-
nutrition brigades in Chiquimula.

## Trigger
No Oxfam-specific trigger exists. The collective framework it plugs into is triggered by ECMWF
SEAS5 seasonal-rainfall forecasts reaching pre-agreed activation thresholds for the Dry Corridor
(Chiquimula, Zacapa, Jalapa and El Progreso departments); once triggered, Oxfam/ASEDECHI
mobilizes nutrition brigades in prioritized Chiquimula municipalities. Public sources describe
Oxfam's implementation role and geographic focus (Jocotán, Camotán) but not a distinct
Oxfam-specific threshold, indicator or lead time — see `extra.schema_strain`.

## Funding & scope
Funded through CERF, channeled via UNICEF as the framework's nutrition-sector lead; the
Oxfam/ASEDECHI-specific dollar amount within the collective ~US$4M July-2025 CERF allocation is
not stated in sources found. Scope: integrated health-and-nutrition brigades staffed by
ASEDECHI-hired nutritionists and community "change agents," working in prioritized communities
of Jocotán and Camotán, Chiquimula (the collective framework's 2025 activation reached
>51,000 people overall across all sectors and agencies).

## Activations
- **Jul 2025 (for the Sep-Nov 2025 season).** ECMWF SEAS5 forecasts confirmed a severe rainfall
  deficit for Chiquimula, triggering the wider OCHA/CERF collective response (~US$4M CERF,
  >51,000 people, food security/health/WASH/nutrition). Oxfam/ASEDECHI's nutrition brigades
  operated in 18 communities across Jocotán and Camotán, screening ~6,000 children and ~4,000
  adolescent girls, pregnant and breastfeeding women; 46 acute-malnutrition cases were
  identified, 60% of which reportedly would not have been detected without the brigades. No
  activation distinct to Oxfam is recorded for 2024 or the March 2026 round (whose OCHA
  reporting names only FAO, PAHO, UNICEF and WFP as implementing agencies).

## Coordination
See `extra.coordination`: this page covers Oxfam/ASEDECHI's nutrition-sector role inside the
OCHA/CERF collective Guatemala drought framework, not an independent mechanism. That collective
framework has no page yet under `frameworks/` in this KB. A separate, independently-triggered
Guatemalan Red Cross EAP for the same country and hazard is documented at
[`external-frameworks/ifrc/gtm-drought.md`](../ifrc/gtm-drought.md) — a different mechanism
(INSIVUMEH/ENSO triggers, DREF funding) that should not be confused with either this page or the
OCHA collective framework.
