# Save the Children — Philippines flood

## Summary
Save the Children Philippines runs "FLood Anticipatory action In Tanay, Rizal" (FLAIR), an
anticipatory-action trial in the flood-prone municipality of Tanay, Rizal — home to many
communities resettled from Manila informal settlements. The project is initially funded by
Australia's Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT) and works with the Tanay local
government unit and the national weather agency PAGASA. It emphasises building disaster
resilience and vulnerability-assessment participation among at-risk groups — persons with
disabilities, women's groups, indigenous groups, and children's groups — rather than
publishing a fully specified forecast-trigger protocol.

## Trigger
Not publicly disclosed in detail. Save the Children's project page names PAGASA as a
partner, implying its flood forecasts/warnings feed the trigger, but no specific
indicator, threshold value, or lead time is published. Save the Children's own global
"Framework for Anticipatory Action" (December 2022) is an organisation-wide guidance
document setting out a common approach across country programmes; it does not itself
specify the Tanay flood trigger.

## Funding & scope
Initially funded by DFAT Australia; no funding amount was found in public sources.
Target population per the Anticipation Hub inventory: 20,143 people. Save the Children's
own project page separately reports a breakdown of girls 4,584 / boys 4,514 / women 6,950
/ men 5,168 (total 21,216) — see `extra.target_population_breakdown` for the
reconciliation gap. Geographic scope is the municipality of Tanay, Rizal (Calabarzon
region).

## Activations
None known. No activation, cash-transfer, or early-action release tied to FLAIR was found
in public sources as of this check.
