# Save the Children — Bangladesh multiple (El Niño drought & heatwave)

## Summary
Save the Children, with the Regional Integrated Multi-Hazard Early Warning
System (RIMES) providing forecasts and local NGO Mahideb Jubo Somaj Kallayan
Somity (MJSKS) implementing on the ground, ran a one-year pilot (stated as June
2023 to early 2024) to test anticipatory action for El Niño-driven drought and
heatwave in Ulipur and Chilmari upazilas of Kurigram district, plus heatwave
awareness activities in Dhaka and two northern districts. The goal was to
"minimize the impacts of El Niño effects to children and families through
piloting early actions for drought and heatwave," targeting an estimated 22,000
people, funded by the Global Humanitarian Fund. It is a standalone Save the
Children pilot, separate from Save the Children's role as a co-financing
partner in OCHA's collective Bangladesh monsoon-floods framework (see
Coordination note below).

## Trigger
The pilot linked RIMES seasonal/heatwave forecasts and Bangladesh Meteorological
Department (BMD) monitoring to flag anticipated drought and heatwave conditions,
triggering pre-agreed community-level early actions: distribution of
drought-tolerant seeds, irrigation-technique demonstrations, and cash support to
the most vulnerable farming families for drought; safe drinking-water
distribution and mass awareness/health-advisory messaging for heatwave. No
public document states a specific numeric threshold (e.g. a temperature or
rainfall-anomaly value) or a forecast lead time in days — the brochure instead
describes the pilot's aim as generating field experience to "harmonize the
threshold & trigger systems and EAs protocol" with the national Anticipatory
Action platform/FbA taskforce, BMD, and Ministry of Disaster Management and
Relief, implying no single finalized numeric trigger was operating publicly by
the pilot's close.

## Funding & scope
Funded by the **Global Humanitarian Fund** (no public USD figure found).
Implemented over **one year** across **Ulipur and Chilmari** upazilas (Kurigram
district), with heatwave-awareness components extending to Dhaka and two
northern districts, targeting an estimated **22,000 people**. Key stakeholders
named in the project brochure: Ministry of Disaster Management and Relief,
Department of Disaster Management, Disaster Management Committees, the Flood
Forecasting and Warning Centre, BMD, RIMES, and BDRCS.

## Activations
No discrete, dated activation (a single trigger-fire event) is documented in
public sources. Instead, the pilot delivered continuous early actions across
its 2023–2024 window: drought-tolerant seed distribution, irrigation
demonstrations, and cash support to roughly 120 of the most vulnerable families
in Kurigram, plus safe drinking-water distribution and awareness campaigns
ahead of and during the severe April 2024 heatwave (temperatures reaching 42°C,
forcing the closure of schools nationwide for 33 million children) — as
reported in Save the Children UK's 25 April 2024 press release. No numbered,
single-date "trigger fired" record was found.
