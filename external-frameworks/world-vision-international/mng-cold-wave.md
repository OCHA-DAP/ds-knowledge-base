# World Vision International — Mongolia cold wave (dzud)

## Summary
World Vision Mongolia runs its own anticipatory-action programme for dzud — the compound
summer-drought/severe-winter hazard that kills livestock and devastates herder
livelihoods — activating cash, food, fodder and non-food-item support to vulnerable herder
households ahead of the typical February-April mortality peak. The mechanism traces to a
2018 Start Fund-financed pilot run jointly with Save the Children, and has since run as
World Vision's own bilaterally-funded (Start Network, Aktion Deutschland Hilft, and World
Vision donor funds) response in at least the 2023 and 2024 dzud seasons, each activated off
national meteorological early-warning bulletins.

## Trigger
World Vision watches the same national early-warning source used across Mongolia's dzud
anticipatory-action landscape: bulletins and the Dzud Risk Map issued by the National Agency
for Meteorology and Environmental Monitoring (NAMEM), which flag the share of national
territory at high/very-high dzud risk. In practice, World Vision activated when NAMEM's
1 February 2023 warning showed ~60% of national territory at high/very-high risk, and again
when its 20 December 2023 warning showed ~90% at high/very-high risk. No public World
Vision document states a fixed numeric threshold (e.g. a set percentage or province count)
or a stated lead-time rule — contrast the neighbouring Mongolian Red Cross Society/IFRC EAP,
which does publish one ("3+ provinces at ≥20% of area very-high risk"), but that is a
different organisation's protocol. World Vision's own reporting ties activation to
community-informed targeting (in 2023, a 360-person consultation process helped shape
selection criteria) reaching at least the 20% most vulnerable households in affected
provinces.

## Funding & scope
The Anticipation Hub's current Mongolia country listing puts World Vision's active framework
at US$534,536 pre-arranged for 74,938 people. Named co-funders across activations are Start
Network/Start Fund, Aktion Deutschland Hilft (Germany — a 15-month project covering the 2023
activation), and World Vision's own child-sponsorship/donor funds; no public source
apportions this by dollar amount (`funding_by_source` left empty). Individual activations
have run smaller: the 2018 pilot allocated GBP 200,000 across 7 districts; the Hub records
the 2023 activation alone at US$132,456 for 7,612 people. See `extra.schema_strain` for how
these figures don't fully reconcile with the framework-level total.

## Activations
- **December 2018 — Start Fund Alert 783** — the original pilot: GBP 200,000 to Save the
  Children and World Vision within 72 hours of a harsh-winter forecast in 4 provinces;
  unconditional cash, livestock fodder/hay and child-protection information to herder
  households across 7 western districts.
- **February 2023** — NAMEM warning (1 Feb) of ~60% of national territory at high/very-high
  risk; World Vision ran a 7-month anticipatory-action/relief/recovery response across 6
  western provinces, reaching 3,400+ herder families (16,000+ people) within 3 months; a
  15-month ADH-funded project underpinned part of this response. Hub-recorded scale:
  US$132,456 for 7,612 people.
- **February-April 2024** — NAMEM warning (20 Dec 2023) of ~90% of national territory at
  high/very-high risk, amid 3.9M+ reported livestock deaths by early March 2024; World
  Vision responded across 16 western provinces plus 2 semi-urban districts, reaching 5,892
  herder families with food, fodder, cash and warm clothing. The Hub's own listing tags this
  year's implementing org as Save the Children rather than World Vision — not reconciled
  with World Vision's own account of directly running the response (see
  `extra.schema_strain`).
