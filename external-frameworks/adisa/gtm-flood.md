# ADISA — Guatemala flood

## Summary
ADISA (Asociación de Padres y Amigos de Personas con Discapacidad), a community-based
disability organisation based around Santiago Atitlán/Sololá and a longtime CBM partner on
rehabilitation and inclusive-education work, has co-developed a disability-inclusive
anticipatory action protocol for floods in Panajachel, on Lake Atitlán, together with CBM,
Asociación Vivamos Mejor, and the Panajachel municipal government. Publicly described as
the first disability-inclusive AA protocol of its kind in the region, it was showcased at
the RIADIS Latin America and Caribbean Regional Pre-Summit on Disability (Rio de Janeiro,
December 2024) and is reported to be expanding to two neighbouring municipalities,
San Marcos la Laguna and San Lucas Tolimán. Public documentation is thin — a single CBM
social-media post is the only source located — so trigger detail, exact scale, and
activation history are largely unknown.

## Trigger
No published indicator, threshold, or lead time was found. The only public description
frames the trigger/action-planning process as collective and locally led: early actions
are "collectively defined" using local data and the active participation of persons with
disabilities, women, and youth representatives, combined with georeferenced mapping of
where persons with disabilities live so responders know what assistance to bring. This is
a design philosophy, not a forecast-based trigger specification (no named hydromet
indicator, e.g. rainfall or lake-level threshold, or a stated lead time, appears in any
source found).

## Funding & scope
The Municipality of Panajachel has committed €40,000 (≈US$43,000 at an approximate
EUR/USD conversion; no official rate stated) toward activation of the protocol —
described as "local funding and ownership" in CBM's reporting. No target-people figure is
published; the protocol is scoped to persons with disabilities, women, and youth in
Panajachel, with pre-positioned resources including accessible shelters, assistive
devices, medicine, and food. The model is reported to be expanding to San Marcos la Laguna
and San Lucas Tolimán, both also on Lake Atitlán in Sololá department.

## Activations
None known. No public record of the protocol having been triggered by an actual flood
event was found.
