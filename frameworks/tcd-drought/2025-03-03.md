# Chad Drought — 2025-03-03

## Summary

The Chad anticipatory action drought framework triggers CERF funding (up to $8M USD) for drought preparedness across seven Sahelian provinces when either SEAS5 seasonal precipitation forecasts signal a dry season ahead (Windows 1 and 2) or end-of-season biomass measurements confirm drought onset (Window 3). The framework has three independent trigger windows, each linked to a distinct intervention package (livelihood protection for main harvest, livelihood protection for market gardening and livestock, and early humanitarian impact mitigation). All three windows can be activated independently; if all fire, the maximum $8M is released. The OCHA Centre for Humanitarian Data (CHD) monitors all triggers and notifies the Humanitarian Coordinator upon threshold exceedance, triggering CERF disbursement.

## Method

**Forecast trigger (Windows 1 and 2):** ECMWF SEAS5 ensemble mean precipitation forecasts are downloaded monthly. For each pixel in the AOI, the current forecast is ranked against the historical distribution (1981–2024) to produce a pixel-wise historical percentile rank. The spatial 20th percentile (i.e., the value exceeded by 80% of AOI pixels) is computed. If this spatial 20th percentile falls below the 15th historical percentile threshold, the trigger fires. Equivalently: at least 20% of the AOI has forecast JAS rainfall in the lowest 15% of historical seasons.

**Observational trigger (Window 3):** Cumulative biomass at the third dekad of August (dekad 24) is computed from ACF/GeoSahel DMP data aggregated over the expanded 7-province AOI. A linear trend over 1999–2024 is fitted to the absolute biomass time series. The anomaly is computed as `actual / linear_fit_prediction * 100`. If this trend-adjusted anomaly is below 84.5%, the trigger fires (a ~5-year return period).

**Combined return period:** The CERF-accepted design target is a 4.5-year overall return period (at least one window fires). Individual Windows 1 and 2 each have ~13-year return periods; Window 3 has ~5.2 years.

## Trigger logic

- **Keys off:**
  - Windows 1 & 2: SEAS5 ECMWF ensemble mean seasonal forecast for JAS precipitation
  - Window 3: ACF/GeoSahel cumulative biomass (ADM1-aggregated, third dekad August), trend-adjusted anomaly

- **Decision rule (plain language):**
  - Window 1 fires if SEAS5 issued in March OR April shows at least 20% of the AOI having forecast JAS rainfall in the lowest 15% of historical years.
  - Window 2 fires if SEAS5 issued in May OR June shows the same condition.
  - Window 3 fires if cumulative biomass by end of August is below 84.5% of what the long-term linear trend predicts for that year (i.e., a drought year relative to trend).

- **Activation structure:** Three independent OR-linked windows. Each window activates individually; a single fired window releases its allocated funds. No AND condition between windows. Within each forecast window, the March or April (Window 1) / May or June (Window 2) issue month fires the window — the most favourable single month suffices.

- **Calibration:** Of all possible threshold combinations, 18 achieved the CERF-specified 4.5-year **overall** RP (Annex 4). The two of those with the most *similar* per-window RPs were then compared, and **Option 1** was chosen because it gave a slightly *higher* activation probability on the observational (biomasse) trigger, whose expected accuracy is greater (`combined_rp_2025.md`, lines 347–348: `display_yearly_activations_fixed_seas5(0.15, 5.4)`). The 15th-percentile SEAS5 threshold is an **output of that sweep**, not a value carried across from IRI — what the revision kept from the 2022 design is the *percentile treatment* of the forecast (Annex 4: absolute SEAS5 rainfall was converted to per-pixel historical percentiles because that "devrait être une meilleure approximation au déclencheur ancien qui utilisait les probabilités terciles"), plus the unchanged 20%-of-area fraction, monitoring months and JAS target season. `analysis/switch_ecmwf.md` compares SEAS5 against IRI (`ORIGINAL_IRI_THRESH = 42.5`) at **matched return periods**, not at a matched percentile. The biomasse 84.5% threshold corrects for an observed positive trend in vegetation productivity; without trend correction the threshold would fail to detect 2017 (a known drought year) while falsely catching 2002.

- **Authoritative source:** 2025-03-03 framework PDF (`framework_doc`). The repo (`code_ref`) implements and derives it.

- **Operated by:** OCHA CHD (null — internal). CHD monitors, verifies with national meteorological service (DREM), and certifies trigger to HC and CERF.

## Key decisions & rationale

**Switch from IRI to SEAS5:** The 2022 pilot used IRI seasonal forecasts (Columbia University) at ≥42.5% probability of below-normal JAS precipitation (tercile). The 2025 revision switched to ECMWF SEAS5 due to "operational uncertainties" in IRI production. To approximate the IRI tercile-probability concept with a continuous data source, SEAS5 ensemble means were converted to pixel-wise historical percentile ranks; the spatial 20th-percentile of this rank field approximates the fraction of the area with forecast in the lowest tercile. Annex 4 is explicit that the *other* parameters — the 20% area fraction, the monitoring months, the JAS target period — stayed the same; the 15th-percentile **value** came from the return-period calibration (the 18 combinations hitting the CERF 4.5-year overall RP → Option 1), **not** from an IRI-equivalence calculation. `analysis/switch_ecmwf.md` is the repo's side-by-side IRI-vs-SEAS5 comparison behind the switch: it recomputes individual/combined return periods for SEAS5 and plots IRI against the retained `ORIGINAL_IRI_THRESH`, i.e. it matches the two sources on **return period**, not on threshold value.

**Trend correction for biomass:** A statistically significant positive trend was found in AOI aggregate biomass (1999–2024). Without trend correction, the 5-year RP threshold would have triggered on 2002 (not a reported drought year) but missed 2017 (CERF rapid response issued). Trend-adjusting corrects this: the 2025 framework uses `actual / linear_fit * 100` with a threshold of 84.5%, replacing the 2022 `≤ 80% of climatological mean` rule.

**AOI expansion:** The geographic scope grew from five provinces (Lac, Kanem, Bahr-El-Gazal, Batha, Wadi Fira) to seven (adding Ouaddaï and Sila) to improve alignment with food insecurity data and the ICC vulnerability analysis (November 2024).

**Biomasse as the observational indicator:** the doc's justification is that the biomasse anomaly published at the start of September is "le meilleur prédicteur des conditions de sécheresse à la fin de la saison" — the earliest reliable read on end-of-season severity, so action can start before the full humanitarian impact manifests. The doc does not publish a head-to-head indicator comparison; the repo's other observational explorations are CHIRPS (`exploration/chirps_monthly.md`) and ERA5 rainfall/dry-spells (`exploration/era5_monthly.md`, `era5_reanalysis.md`). There is **no WRSI comparison in this repo** (WRSI appears only in a `biomasse.load_biomasse_mean` docstring about masking).

**Target return period:** The CERF committed to a 4.5-year **overall** return period — i.e. at least one window fires (releasing that window's partial envelope) on average once every 4.5 years. This is **not** the same as spending the full $8M every 4.5 years: because this is a split-budget framework, the **effective** RP (how often the *maximum* $8M is spent, which needs the windows to fire together) is longer — the doc states it is "by definition ≥ the overall return period" (framework doc, RP section) without printing a number in the text; from the doc's own backtest and window budgets it works out at ≈**7.3 years** (mean annual spend $1.10M, see `extra.return_period_effective_yrs_derived`). Vocabulary and the individual/overall/effective relations: [methods/return-periods.md](../../methods/return-periods.md). The design explores 18 combinations meeting the overall-RP target (18 of all possible threshold combinations achieved the CERF-required RP, per Annex 4) and selects the one with the most balanced individual window RPs and the highest expected accuracy of the observational trigger.
