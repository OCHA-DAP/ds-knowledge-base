# Bangladesh Tropical Cyclone — 2025-04-25

> The canonical trigger is the 2025 framework PDF (`framework_doc`); this page explains it, it does not redefine it. The repo (`code_ref`) is a Shiny app that implements the union-level wind-speed step for the monitoring team — it does not contain the analysis behind the trigger, and the PDF never names it (see `discrepancies`).

## Summary

The Bangladesh cyclone anticipatory action framework, initiated in January 2023 and revised to its current form in 2025 (the exec summary says "revised in March 2025"; the cover page records RC approval 7 April 2025 and USG/ERC endorsement 25 April 2025, which dates this version), pre-arranges funding and activities for multi-sectoral humanitarian response ahead of severe cyclones making landfall on Bangladesh's Bay of Bengal coast. It activates when at least two of five forecast models agree that wind speeds will reach 118 km/h (Category I, ~1-in-4.75-year return period) in the six target coastal districts — Satkhira, Patuakhali, Bhola, Noakhali, Barguna, and Khulna — within a 3-to-5-day window (readiness) or up to 72-hour window (action). A third, observational trigger covers the scenario where a cyclone reaches threshold wind speed at landfall without having triggered the forecast-based stages; associated early-response activities can run for up to ~4 weeks after landfall. The pre-arranged CERF envelope is USD 4 million, alongside USD 5,951,987 in partner co-funding (BDRCS, BRAC, STEP, UNFPA, UNICEF, Islamic Relief, Save the Children, Start Network, WFP) — the doc headlines the combined USD 9,951,987 as the "total pre-arranged amount", but this page splits CERF from co-financing per the KB convention (`prearranged_funding_usd` / `cofinancing_usd`), so the headline is preserved at `extra.doc_stated_total_prearranged_usd`. The co-funding line items in the same table sum to $6,015,987, a $64,000 gap against the printed $5,951,987 subtotal that the document does not explain (see discrepancies). The four CERF-funded UN implementing agencies are FAO, UNFPA, UNICEF, and WFP. The framework has not been activated for a real cyclone event.

## Method

During cyclone seasons (April–June and October–November), a three-person monitoring team tracks five forecast systems — BMD, IMD, ECMWF, GFS, and JTWC — twice daily. When a cyclone threat develops, the team applies a wind reduction factor (`0.9807 * exp(-0.003 * distance_km)`, developed under the IFRC DREF EAP Cyclone analysis) to the forecast landfall wind speed to estimate expected wind speed at each of the union-level (ADM4) administrative units in the six target districts. In practice this calculation is operationalised in the Shiny monitoring app (`app.R`), which computes distance from the user-supplied landfall point to each union's polygon **boundary** (UTM 46N / EPSG:32646), not its centroid — note the PDF describes the steps abstractly and never names the app, so the app's role (and its boundary-distance choice) is inferred from the repo, not documented. The team counts model agreements and checks against the 118 km/h threshold at union level to determine whether the readiness, action, or observational trigger criteria are met, then sends a standardised email (plus WhatsApp) to the distribution list including the RC, HCTT members, UN agencies, OCHA and CERF.

## Trigger logic

- **Keys off:** Multi-model wind speed forecast consensus (BMD, IMD, ECMWF, GFS, JTWC) at the union level in six coastal districts, using a wind-reduction-factor model applied to the forecast landfall wind speed; a well-formed low-pressure/depression system in the Bay of Bengal for Stage II; observed wind speed at landfall for Stage III.
- **Decision rule (plain language):** If at least two of the five listed forecast models predict wind speeds of 118 km/h or higher in the coastal pilot districts with 3–5 days of lead time, the readiness trigger fires and CERF disburses the full amount to partners (who begin readiness activities only). If the same two-model consensus holds with up to 72 hours of lead time and a well-formed low pressure or depression has formed in the Bay, the action trigger fires and full implementation begins immediately, no further approval required. If a readiness trigger was missed but the action trigger is reached, disbursement and implementation both start at that point. If the **action** trigger did not fire but observed wind at landfall reaches 118 km/h (confirmed by BMD/IMD, or by consensus of at least two of ECMWF/GFS/JTWC), the observational trigger fires and 100% of funds can be spent; early-response activities under this trigger can run for up to ~4 weeks after landfall, with geographic reprioritisation if the actually-impacted area differs from the six CERF districts. The doc notes there is a 50% chance the readiness trigger is not reached while the action trigger is (or vice versa) — the two are independent checks, not a strict staged funnel.
- **Activation structure:** Three sequential stages — Stage I (Readiness, 3–5 days), Stage II (Action, ≤72 hours), Stage III (Observational, at/after landfall, early response window up to ~4 weeks). The observational trigger can only fire if the action trigger did not. *Provenance note:* the ~4-week early-response window comes from the doc's **AA activation workflow diagram** (p.3, "Phase 3 – Early Response (up to 4 weeks after landfall)"), not from its prose — so it is in the slide-visual/captions sidecar (`raw/frameworks/bgd-cyclone/2025-04-25.captions.txt`) and **not** greppable in the plain-text `raw_extract`. The same diagram puts Phase 2 at "0–3 days", corroborating that the action trigger has no lower lead-time cutoff.
- **Edge cases:** activation with <24 hours' lead time is left to agency/RC discretion on whether it is safe to implement (vs. shifting to early response); agencies must otherwise observe government-mandated facility closures starting 12 hours before landfall.
- **Calibration:** The 118 km/h threshold corresponds to a 1-in-4.75-year return period based on historical cyclone data for the Bangladesh coast (1977–2023, per the district-ranking table; the Annex 1 event table spans 1991–2023). At this wind speed, structural damage modelling (BDRCS/GRC survey of ~200 rural households) indicates ~30% (central zone) / ~25% (western zone) household asset damage — generalised to "≥25% of houses damaged" — or ≥40% of union population affected.
- **Authoritative source:** 2025 framework PDF (`framework_doc`). The Shiny app (`app.R`) implements the union-level wind speed calculation for the monitoring team; it does not make a go/no-go decision automatically.
- **Operated by:** OCHA/CHD (monitoring team) and the AA-TWG. The RC, in consultation with MoDMR, can pause an activation in outlier cases where a storm track would clearly cause minimal impact; no external platform (IRI, INSIVUMEH, etc.) runs the trigger.

## Trigger windows

**n_windows = 3** (one per stage; all stages share the same 118 km/h threshold and geographic scope — they differ only in lead time and data basis).

| window | basis | indicator | threshold | lead time | return period | releases |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stage I — Readiness | forecast | ≥2 of 5 models forecast wind speed at union level | 118 km/h | 3–5 days before landfall | ~1-in-4.75 years | CERF disburses full amount; partners begin readiness activities only |
| Stage II — Action | forecast | ≥2 of 5 models forecast wind speed at union level; well-formed low pressure or depression formed in Bay | 118 km/h | up to 72 hours before landfall (no lower cutoff in the trigger statement; see Edge cases) | ~1-in-4.75 years | Partners begin full implementation of all activities, no further approval needed |
| Stage III — Observational | observational | Observed wind at landfall (BMD/IMD) or ≥2 of {ECMWF, GFS, JTWC} consensus | 118 km/h | at landfall/crossover; early response window up to ~4 weeks post-landfall | ~1-in-4.75 years | Agencies "authorised to spend 100% of the funds" (doc wording — spend authorisation, not a further release); geographic reprioritisation to actual impact area may occur |

## Monitoring

The three-person monitoring team (Ahmadul Hassan, Pauline Ndirangu, Leonardo Milano) monitors forecasts twice daily during cyclone seasons (April–June and October–November) using the Shiny app (`app.R`). The app accepts a landfall location and forecast wind speed as input, applies the wind reduction formula to compute expected wind speed at each ADM4 union in the six target districts, and highlights unions above the (user-editable, default 118 km/h) threshold on a Leaflet map and in summary/detail tables (with CSV download). Results are communicated via standardised email and WhatsApp to the distribution list (RC, HCTT members, UN agencies, OCHA, CERF), including an intervention map. The monitoring app is containerised (image `shiny-azure-app-service`) and runs on Azure App Service — live at [`ds-aa-bgd-cyclone-monitoring`](https://ds-aa-bgd-cyclone-monitoring-axc8gdejhwfrd6hy.eastus2-01.azurewebsites.net) on the shared `DsciAppServicePlan` (see `infrastructure/deployments.md`). The GHA workflow `container-registry.yaml` only **builds and pushes** the image to Azure Container Registry on every push to `master` — there is no deploy step in the repo, so the App Service ↔ image binding (and whether it is a prod or dev slot) is configured outside the repo. The Dockerfile and `R/lines.R` carry commented-out DEV blob-SAS build args (`DSCI_AZ_BLOB_DEV_SAS`), residue from development against the dev storage environment; see `discrepancies`.

There is no automated monitoring pipeline — the monitoring team manually inputs forecast wind speeds and landfall location from the five model sources into the app. Trigger confirmation is a human judgment call against the multi-model consensus rule.

## Historical activations

**Never activated** (as of `last_synced` 2026-08-31). This version has been endorsed since 25 April 2025; the predecessor was endorsed 23 December 2023 (revision endorsed 17 May 2024). No real cyclone has met the trigger criteria in the target districts during either period.

The closest case was **Tropical Cyclone Remal** (landfall 26 May 2024, under the prior version), which struck the target coastal districts. The CERF allocation report is explicit that "during the monitoring period (April–May), the trigger conditions required for disbursing AA funds were not met" — so the AA trigger never fired. Four days after landfall, on **30 May 2024**, the ERC instead approved a **rapid-response** allocation to FAO/UNFPA/UNICEF/WFP, and the RC subsequently had the existing AA *projects* repurposed for post-disaster relief. Two things worth keeping straight: (a) the report states the RR funds "did not impact the pre-arranged AA funding, which remained intact" — the AA envelope was not spent; and (b) the report itself quotes the allocation inconsistently as **$7.4M / $7.5M / $7.62M** in different sections, while its own Table 2 (CERF funding by project and sector) totals **$7,415,323** — use the table figure. This was **not** a trigger-based activation of this framework and is therefore not in `activations`. (Sources: [CERF report 24-RR-BGD-63521](https://cerf.un.org/sites/default/files/resources/24-RR-BGD-63521_Bangladesh_CERF_Report.pdf), [CHD](https://centre.humdata.org/how-ochas-anticipatory-approach-to-predictable-shocks-is-speeding-up-post-disaster-response/); the 2025 framework PDF itself does not mention Remal.)

The 2025 PDF lists historical cyclones for context (Annex 1, Table 1, 1991–2023): events exceeding 118 km/h in the Bay of Bengal include Bob 1 (1991, 250 km/h), Sidr (2007, 223 km/h), Aila (2009, 120 km/h), Mora (2017, 146 km/h), Bulbul (2019, 140 km/h), Amphan (2020, 151 km/h), Yaas (2021, ~150 km/h), and Mocha (2023, 145 km/h) — eight events, not the six the doc's own Risk/Hazard narrative claims (see discrepancies). These are for return-period calibration only — none activated this framework.

## Key decisions & rationale

**118 km/h threshold:** Chosen as the wind speed at which structural damage modelling (BDRCS/GRC household survey, ~200 homes) predicts ~30% asset damage in the central zone and ~25% in the western zone (PDF Table 2; note the table's own per-wall-type cells do not cleanly reconcile to those weighted averages — see `discrepancies`), generalised to "≥25% of houses damaged", and/or ≥40% of union population affected. This also aligns with the national EAP threshold approved by the Government of Bangladesh and the IFRC/BDRCS EAP.

**Multi-model consensus (≥2 of 5):** The doc gives **no stated rationale** for the 2-of-5 rule itself — "reduces false alarms from any single model" is a plausible reading, not something the PDF argues. What the doc *does* state: ECMWF additionally shows landfall likelihood, "which is useful for activation decisions"; per Annex 1 Table 3 (skill by model, sourced to the IFRC EAP Cyclone), IMD and JTWC forecasts are good after cyclonic-storm (CS) formation and ECMWF/GFS after depression (DD) formation, while BMD wind skill is good from ~1,000 km out. The lead-time split is explicitly justified by the *unnumbered forecast-error table* that follows Table 3 (not Table 3 itself): "the model track forecast beyond 48 hours the error is very high. Hence can be used only for readiness but not for activation" — i.e. long-lead forecasts are trusted enough to release money and get ready, but not to start implementation.

**Three-stage design:** Readiness at 3–5 days maximises lead time for pre-positioning and beneficiary registration; action at ≤72 hours is when forecast skill is deemed sufficient for activation; the observational trigger provides a backstop (with an early-response window of up to ~4 weeks post-landfall) for cases where the storm strengthens unexpectedly near landfall.

**CERF pre-arrangement:** The RC and ERC pre-approve the CERF allocation ($4M) so that funds are released automatically upon trigger — no case-by-case approval needed, on a no-regrets basis, once endorsed and pre-approved. CERF disburses even if only the readiness trigger fires, but partners hold action-phase funds until the action trigger is reached. CERF funding must be triggered within two years of the ERC's pre-approval (25 April 2025 → drives `valid_until` 2027-04-25).

**Financing structure** — the doc's Sec 4 "Financing overview" table verbatim. Its grand total ($9,951,987) is the doc's own combined "total pre-arranged" headline; this page records the CERF column as `prearranged_funding_usd` ($4M) and the co-funding column as `cofinancing_usd` ($5,951,987), per the KB convention.

**Geographic scope:** Six CERF-funded districts (Satkhira, Patuakhali, Bhola, Noakhali, Barguna, Khulna) are the top-ranked by a composite INFORM RISK (natural hazard + vulnerability) + cyclone-frequency score (1977–2023); the next two ranked districts, Pirojpur and Bagerhat, are in scope for the wider 8-district sea-facing area but not CERF-funded. The framework now allows AA activities across all eight sea-facing districts for co-funded partners, but CERF is restricted to the top six.

**Wind reduction formula:** Derived from the IFRC DREF EAP Cyclone for Bangladesh. The app hardcodes `0.9807 * exp(-0.003 * distance_km)` to estimate wind speed at a union given distance to its polygon boundary (not centroid) — a methodological improvement over the 2023 centroid-based approach, though the exact constants don't precisely match either of the two per-cyclone fitted curves shown in the PDF's own Annex 1 Graph 1 (see discrepancies).

## Changes from previous version

The 2025 version (RC 7 April 2025, USG/ERC 25 April 2025) supersedes the 2023 version (RC 11 September 2023, USG/ERC 23 December 2023; revision approved 13 May 2024 and endorsed 17 May 2024). All comparisons below were verified against the public 2023 PDF at `extra.prior_version_doc`. Key changes:

1. **Dropping of the BMD Signal branch (a simplification, not the removal of a hard requirement):** The 2023 trigger stated each stage as **two alternative branches** — (i) "BMD issues a Signal 1 (squally weather) or above" *AND* ≥2-of-5 models at 118 km/h with 3–5 days lead (readiness), or "BMD issues a Signal 5 or above" *AND* the model consensus at 48–72 hours (action); **or** (ii) the model consensus alone at the same lead times. The 2025 version keeps only branch (ii). So the BMD Signal was never a blocking AND-condition on the only path to activation — a models-only path already existed in 2023 — and 2025 simply retires the redundant Signal-based branch. Worth noting because the doc's own narrative does not flag the change at all.

2. **Addition of Stage III Observational Trigger:** A genuinely new post-landfall backstop trigger, with an early-response window of up to ~4 weeks after landfall. It activates if the cyclone reaches 118 km/h at landfall without having triggered Stages I or II, covering the "rapid intensification near coast" scenario. The 2023 PDF contains no observational trigger of any kind (the string "observational" does not appear in it) — this is the largest structural addition in 2025.

3. **Return period adjustment:** From "a 1 in 5-year return period or more" (2023) to "a 1-in-4.75-year return period or greater" (2025). The threshold wind speed (118 km/h) and the "25% household asset damage" impact anchor are unchanged; neither document explains the shift, and 2025 additionally adds the "≥40% of the population in a union likely to be affected" alternative.

4. **Action-trigger cutoff softened:** 2023 set an explicit hard floor — "the cutoff time for activation has been set at 48 hours ahead of landfall", with sub-48-hour scenarios handled by the RC and "no automatic activation of the activities expected". 2025 states only "a lead time of up to 72 hours" with no lower cutoff in the trigger statement, moving the sub-24-hour case into a discretionary edge-case section and adding the government-mandated 12-hour pre-landfall facility-closure rule.

5. **Funding: de-pooling *plus* a smaller cyclone envelope, and co-funding roughly flat.** In 2023 CERF provided a single **$7.5M envelope shared with the monsoon-floods framework** — "the same pool of funds will be used for both projects so if one is triggered additional funding would need to be re-secured" — against which the cyclone framework's own CERF table requested **$7,430,821**. In 2025 the cyclone framework gets a **ring-fenced $4M**, and the 2025 floods framework a separate ring-fenced $6M, so total Bangladesh CERF AA rises from $7.5M (competing) to $10M (non-competing) while the **cyclone-specific ceiling falls from ~$7.43M to $4M**. Co-funding is close to flat: the 2023 table sums to **$5,816,794** (no printed total) vs 2025's printed **$5,951,987** (line items $6,015,987). The partner mix churned rather than expanded — **Save the Children ($150K)** and **BRAC ($8.5K)** are new and **UNICEF co-funding ($1.9M)** is new; **Start Network ($755K→$1.775M)** and **Islamic Relief ($45K→$64K)** grew but were already 2023 co-funders; **WFP fell $3.0M→$1.6M** and **UNFPA fell $806,330→$15,930**; and **Care International ($275K), Christian Aid ($115K), Concern Worldwide ($55K)** and **FAO's SFERA contribution ($179K)** dropped out entirely. The 2023 figures are in `extra.prior_version_funding`.

6. **Geographic scope widened for co-funded partners:** 2025 records that the framework "initially targeted only the top five districts" and now permits AA activities "across all sea-facing districts" (eight in the ranking table), while CERF money stays limited to the top six most exposed.

7. **Monitoring app:** A new Shiny monitoring app (`ds-aa-bgd-cyclone-monitoring`) operationalises the union-level wind speed calculation, and changed the distance basis from the 2023 PDF's union **centroid** to the union polygon **boundary** (see discrepancies). No equivalent tool was documented in the 2023 version — and the 2025 PDF does not document this one either.

## Open questions / known issues

- **Budget inconsistency in 2025 PDF:** Executive summary states $8,026,987 total; the Sec 4 financing table states $9,951,987; that table's own line items sum to $10,015,987. None of the three agree. Notably $8,026,987 is *exactly* the line-item sum minus Start Network + Islamic Relief + Save the Children, which supports reading the exec summary as an uncorrected draft value predating those three co-funders. The $64,000 gap has a matching explanation: $5,951,987 = the exec-summary six-partner subtotal ($4,026,987) + Start Network ($1,775,000) + Save the Children ($150,000), i.e. the printed total was recomputed when those two rows were added but not when the Islamic Relief row was. Both readings point to $6,015,987 being the true co-funding figure, but that's an inference from the arithmetic — recommend confirming the total with OCHA/CHD before changing `cofinancing_usd` off the doc's printed number. Only the CERF $4M is unambiguous.
- **Annex 4 sector split is one confirmation away from usable:** the arithmetic says the "Protection – GBV" row ($88,235 CERF + $15,930 co-funding) is UNFPA's, not UNICEF's ($211,765 + $88,235 = UNFPA's exact $300,000 CERF total; $15,930 = UNFPA's exact co-funding). If OCHA/CHD confirms that reading, `funding_by_sector` can be populated — except that the corrected column still totals $3,999,000 against the $4M envelope, so the residual $1,000 needs explaining too. Until then `funding_by_sector` stays `{}` rather than shipping a dict that fails the additive cross-check.
- **Prior version is a stub, not an ingestion:** `supersedes: "2023-12-23"` now resolves — `frameworks/bgd-cyclone/2023-12-23.md` was backfilled 2026-08-25 — but it is a doc-link stub that explicitly says "trigger details have not been extracted". The 2023 PDF is public (`extra.prior_version_doc`) and was read in full for this review, and its trigger, financing table and monitoring steps are all captured here (*Changes from previous version*, `extra.prior_version_funding`). Cheap win: promote that material onto the 2023 page so the version history stands on its own rather than only existing as a diff on this page.
- **No analysis repo anywhere:** The calibration underlying the 118 km/h / 1-in-4.75-year return period, the INFORM district ranking, and the wind-reduction-formula constants are not reproducible from any ocha-dap repo — the derivation is attributed to the external IFRC DREF EAP Cyclone document, which is not linked in the `framework_doc` chain. All that survives on the OCHA side is the PDF narrative and annex graphs. If the threshold or formula needs updating (e.g. after a future activation and evaluation), there is no code to update. If the analysis does exist in a private or unpublished repo, it should be linked here.
- **Where does the -0.003 decay constant come from?** Neither annex-graph regression gives it (Amphan -0.002, Mocha -0.037). Someone chose or blended it; the reasoning is recorded nowhere.
- **App is undocumented and self-labelled "in development":** The 2025 PDF never mentions the Shiny app, so its operational role rests on inference. Meanwhile the app UI still shows "This tool is in development". Either the doc should name the tool the monitoring steps depend on, or the label should go — right now neither side of the pair is authoritative.
- **Threshold editability in app:** The trigger threshold is user-editable in the Shiny app UI (default 118, `app.R:138`). Operationally useful for scenario testing, but it creates a risk of accidental non-standard threshold use during a real monitoring event.
- **Observational trigger geography:** The PDF notes that if the observational trigger fires, the target districts may differ from those actually impacted, requiring geographic reprioritisation. The monitoring app does not currently implement any observational wind data input — it is purely forecast-based. How observational trigger monitoring (and the ~4-week early-response window) is operationalised is not documented in the repo.
