# Nigeria Flood — 2025-08-11 (superseded)

> **Superseded** by [2026-06-18](2026-06-18.md). This page documents the framework as endorsed by the ERC on 11 August 2025 — the version that **activated on 8 September 2025** and released the full $7M envelope. Its trigger is no longer the live one: the successor's multi-gauge design landed on `main` via PR #37 (merged 2026-08-10), so the code at `source_sha` implements *that* design, not this one. For what runs today, read [2026-06-18](2026-06-18.md).
>
> The canonical trigger is the code at `code_ref`; this page explains it, it does not redefine it.

## Summary

This framework pre-arranged $7 million (CERF $5 million + NHF $2 million) for flood anticipatory action in Nigeria across two trigger components: (1) a **riverine** flooding forecast trigger keying off GloFAS and Google Flood Hub discharge forecasts on the Benue River upstream of Adamawa state, releasing the full CERF + NHF envelope to 7 LGAs (Fufore, Yola South, Yola North, Girei, Demsa, Numan, Lamurde); and (2) a **flash** flooding observational trigger using FloodScan-derived flood exposure, which can release NHF funds only — not as a second budget, but by reprogramming the same $2 million to 6 BAY-states LGAs (Bama, Bade, Karasuwa, Madagali, Maiduguri, Ngala) under four mutually-exclusive scenarios. It targeted up to 350,000 people and was valid for two years (2025–2026), with CERF committed through 2026 and NHF expected to be spent by end 2025. **The riverine trigger fired on 8 September 2025**, releasing the whole $7M — the framework's only activation. It has since been superseded by [2026-06-18](2026-06-18.md).

## Method

> The Method / Trigger logic / Trigger windows sections below describe **this version's** design from the framework PDF (authoritative per `trigger_source: framework_doc`). It is the design that was live from 11 August 2025 until the successor replaced it, and the one that fired on 8 September 2025. The successor's design is on [2026-06-18](2026-06-18.md) and is not restated here.

**Riverine trigger:** GloFAS reanalysis (historical) and ensemble forecast data are retrieved daily from the Copernicus Climate Data Store (CDS) for the Wuroboki station on the Benue River. Google Flood Hub (GRRR model) forecasts are retrieved via the Google Flood Forecasting API for gauge `hybas_1120842550`. Both discharge values are compared against calibrated thresholds. If either source exceeds its threshold — within the respective lead-time window — the riverine trigger fires and CERF + NHF funds are released immediately.

**Flash flooding trigger:** FloodScan SFED satellite flood extent (3-day rolling average) is overlaid with WorldPop 2020 UN-adjusted population to produce per-LGA flood exposure estimates. The population raster is the **1 km** UN-adjusted grid (`nga_ppp_2020_1km_Aggregated_UNadj.tif`, `src/datasources/worldpop.py`); the PDF's footnote states only "WorldPop 2020 UN-adjusted", with no resolution. These estimates are compared to per-LGA thresholds calibrated to an approximately 4-year return period. If any LGA threshold is exceeded, NHF funds can be reprogrammed.

**Threshold calibration (riverine):** Both model thresholds were calibrated against FloodScan wet-season annual maxima as observational ground truth (the repo analysis works over an August–November wet season), using a grid search over all (GloFAS rank, Google rank) pairs with an OR-logic trigger. Three FloodScan benchmark severities were explored — 3-, 4- and 5-year RP events — and the selected specification was assessed against the **overall 4.5-year return period specified by CERF**. The chosen "balanced trigger" (equal probability of either source activating) sets both sources to approximately a 5.4-year implied return period individually, so that the combined OR-logic lands at that ~4.5-year overall RP. The 5.4-year individual figure is **repo-sourced** (`exploration/2025/combined_gf_grrr_reanalysis.md`, "based on reading of above df, we want individual RPs 5.4 years"); only the ~4.5-year overall RP appears in the PDF. Historical lead times of 14–25 days were observed in reanalysis; the operational GloFAS action window is ≤5 days, and Google's effective lead time is 6–7 days (issued with 1–2 day lag, maximum 8-day forecast).

The PDF's own "Trigger mechanism statistics" table (Annex) states the combined riverine trigger's return period as **1-in-4.5 years** with an **annual activation probability of 22%**, and lists **historical activation years 1998, 1999, 2003, 2012, 2019, 2022** over an analysis window of 1998–2023 (the same historical record the repo's grid search draws on, not an independent figure). A separate accuracy-metrics table validates the trigger against two FloodScan flood-extent severity benchmarks: **accuracy 87% / detection rate 47% / false alarm rate 10% / precision 60% / F1 80%** at the 5-yr RP benchmark, and **accuracy 81% / detection rate 60% / false alarm rate 10% / precision 60% / F1 55%** at the 4-yr RP benchmark. (The Annex prose describes the pair as "3-yr and 5-yr" while the table's own column headers read 5-yr and 4-yr — a PDF-internal inconsistency; the table headers are treated as authoritative for the numbers above. See `discrepancies`.)

**Threshold calibration (flash flood):** FloodScan exposure thresholds were set to give equal probability of triggering in each LGA, with an overall ~4-year return period.

## Trigger logic

- **Keys off:** GloFAS ensemble forecast discharge (CDS `cems-glofas-forecast`, `system_version: operational`, `ensemble_perturbed_forecasts`) at Wuroboki (G5004); Google Flood Hub GRRR model discharge at gauge `hybas_1120842550`; FloodScan SFED 3-day rolling average flood extent × WorldPop 2020 population.
- **Decision rule (plain language):** The riverine trigger fires if either GloFAS predicts ≥3,132 m³/s at Wuroboki within the next 5 days, OR the Google forecast predicts ≥1,195 m³/s at the Kangli/Wuroboki gauge at any lead time. Either source alone is sufficient ("OR" logic). The reanalysis (leadtime=0) can also trigger. The flash flooding trigger fires if FloodScan-derived flood exposure in any of the 6 BAY LGAs exceeds its per-LGA threshold.
- **Activation structure:** Two triggers, but **not co-equal**. The riverine trigger is the framework's activation trigger: it releases CERF + NHF. The flash trigger is headed **"Flash flooding trigger (monitoring only)"** in the PDF — "this framework will also monitor flash flooding **to inform reprogramming options** for the NHF and other partner-specific early/rapid response mobilization" — so it does not itself release the envelope; it informs which NHF reprogramming scenario applies. (This is the main textual support for `all_in: true`; see `extra.all_in_note`.)
- **Calibration:** Return-period based. Riverine: ~4.5-year combined OR-logic RP — the overall RP CERF specified — with each source at ~5.4 yr individually. Flash: ~4-year RP overall, thresholds set to equalise triggering probability across the 6 LGAs.
- **Authoritative source:** The framework PDF (`framework_doc`), endorsed by the ERC 11 August 2025. `exploration/2025/` in the repo holds the analysis that derived it.
- **Operated by:** null — monitoring was operated by OCHA CHD, with the trigger sources reviewed by the government-chaired Trigger Task Team (NiHSA, NiMet, NEMA, NASRDA, OCHA). The check ran in this repo via GHA (`monitoring.yml`, daily at 20:00 UTC); that same workflow now evaluates the successor design instead — see [2026-06-18](2026-06-18.md).

## Key decisions & rationale

**OR logic for riverine trigger:** Both GloFAS and Google GRRR were found to offer complementary skill. The OR logic maximizes F1 score and detection rate while maintaining lead time; either source alone can trigger. This also provides redundancy against data-source disruption.

**Dual-source calibration (~5.4 yr each, ~4.5 yr combined):** CERF specified an **overall 4.5-year** return period for the trigger. Because OR logic makes the combined trigger fire more often than either source alone, each source had to be set *less* sensitive than the target — ~5.4 yr individually — so that the union lands at ~4.5 yr. (An earlier draft of this page attributed a "5-year RP requirement" to CERF; the repo analysis is explicit that 4.5 years is the CERF-specified overall figure, and 5-yr was one of three FloodScan benchmark severities explored.)

**GloFAS 5-day operational window:** GloFAS forecasts extend to day 5 (with day 1 = the forecast issue date). The GloFAS reanalysis can also trigger (leadtime=0). The maximum Google effective lead time is 6–7 days (8-day forecast minus 1–2 day issuance lag).

**Flash flood trigger (NHF only):** Flash flooding is observational (FloodScan SFED) — no reliable forecast source exists at the LGA level. Per-LGA thresholds were set to equalize triggering probability across LGAs. The trigger releases only NHF funds; CERF is reserved for riverine (forecast-based) triggers only.

**NHF reprogramming flexibility:** The NHF $2M is designed to be flexible: if the riverine trigger doesn't fire by mid-September, funds can be reprogrammed to flash-flood response or broader humanitarian priorities (4 explicit scenarios in the framework doc).

**Geographic targeting:** The 7 riverine LGAs in Adamawa state were selected through a four-tier analysis: historical flood/impact data (NiHSA/NiMet), historical flood risk (NEMA), historical average population exposed (FloodScan), and severity of needs (2025 HNRP PiN figures). The 6 flash-flood LGAs were selected based on NEMA flash flood risk mapping and NGO implementation capacity.

## Changes from previous version

This is the **first** endorsed version of the Nigeria flood framework (`supersedes: null`). The 2024 NHF allocation — $2M on a no-regrets basis for readiness, anticipatory action and early response to riverine floods, flash floods and cholera in two of Nigeria's 774 LGAs, Bama (Borno) and Numan (Adamawa), with ~276,600 people estimated exposed — was a precursor pilot, not a formal AA framework with defined triggers; the doc notes that large-scale dam-break floods arrived while that allocation was still being processed, so it functioned as a catalyst for early *response*.
