# Niger drought — 2024-10-24

## Summary
National drought AA framework keyed on seasonal rainfall forecasts for the July–September (JAS) season over Niger south of 17°N, with an observational SPI backstop. Two intervention windows ("save the harvest" / "mitigate impact") tied to distinct activity packages across seven UN agencies (FAO, WFP, UNICEF, UNFPA, UNHCR, WHO, UNDP) and six sectors; CERF committed USD 14.85M (headline ceiling "up to $15M") over an ERC-approved period. Update of the 2022 pilot (which activated in Aug 2022), incorporating lessons learned into an improved trigger, streamlined activities, and additional agency co-financing.

## Method
IRI seasonal forecast probabilities of below-average JAS rainfall (the PDF does not name a Maproom model; the repo's Maproom data export uses the **OCHA Certification** model at Frequency 35%) are averaged over the south-of-17°N zone of interest and compared against the historical forecast distribution — the forecast produces a probability per pixel, and the area average is used. An observational June–July SPI (ENACTS, 5km resolution — DMN station data fused with CHIRP satellite rainfall, CHIRP substituted when station data is missing for ≥20% of stations at 7 August) provides a second, later path, computed on 1 June–31 July cumulative rainfall. Activation auto-releases CERF for the corresponding window's package once the Humanitarian Coordinator and ERC have approved the framework and agency project proposals.

## Trigger logic
- **Keys off:** IRI forecast prob. of below-average JAS rainfall (forecast); June–July SPI (observational, ENACTS/CHIRP).
- **Decision rule:** within a window, two *consecutive* monthly forecasts must both land in the driest 35% of the historical forecast distribution (AND); across the pairs within a window = OR. Window 2 also has an observational path (Jun–Jul SPI in the lowest 35%); per the PDF's Annex 1, this observational path only activates Window 2 **if it hasn't already been activated via the forecast path** — it's a sequential fallback, not a simultaneous independent check.
- **Activation structure:** two windows, each with its own CERF envelope (`all_in: false`). Window 1 = forecast only (one pair from Jan+Feb / Feb+Mar). Window 2 = forecast path (one pair from Mar+Apr / Apr+May / May+Jun) **OR** the Jun–Jul observational SPI, the latter usable only if the forecast path hasn't already activated Window 2. Within a pair the two monthly forecasts must **both** clear the threshold (AND); across pairs within a window it is OR. The doc places activation decisions in Feb–Jun (forecast) and Aug (SPI).
- **Calibration:** the 35% threshold corresponds to roughly a 1-in-3-year single-test frequency for a "severe" drought (worse than 65% of recorded rainy seasons — "les 10 pires années depuis 1991"). Historical backtest, PDF Annex 1: against the technical partners' severe-years list over **1998–2022**, detection rate 71% (i.e. 71% of historically-severe years would have activated Window 1, Window 2 or both; 29% missed) and false-alarm rate 28%; over **1998–2023** the trigger overall would have activated in 38% of years (10 of 26). The consecutive-two-month persistence requirement (to suppress false alarms) was added in July 2023; the observational SPI arm was added to the design in July 2022.
- **Authoritative source:** 2024 framework PDF (Maproom model). The repo is an exploratory re-derivation/calibration explorer, not the production system.
- **Operated by:** the live trigger is **IRI Maproom 2**, operated with WFP/AGRHYMET/DMN/DNPGCA — *not* this repo.

## Monitoring
IRI updates the Maproom analysis by the 22nd of each month Jan–Jun and by 10 August (DMN shares station rainfall data with IRI by 7 August for the June–July SPI), reporting trigger status (met/not met) to OCHA and participating agencies; once the threshold is met the framework is considered automatically activated for the corresponding activity package. No OCHA-run monitoring pipeline in this repo — the operational surface is IRI's. The repo does host a stakeholder-facing calibration explorer (marimo, WASM-exported, live at `https://ocha-dap.github.io/ds-aa-ner-drought/` with a locked static snapshot at defaults forecast 35% / obs 15% under `/static/`), but it was built in July 2026 as part of the 2026-06-03 successor work and is a calibration tool, not a deployment of this version — it is listed under `apps:` on that page.

## Historical activations
**2022 pilot activation (real):** the framework's 2022-08-04 pilot version activated in August 2022 — Jun–Jul 2022 SPI in south-west Niger was among the lowest in 30 years, triggering the observational arm; ~USD 9.5M was approved by the CERF Emergency Relief Coordinator; ~160,069 people were assisted with nutrition, agriculture, education, WASH, health, and protection (incl. GBV) activities. This predates and does not fall under this 2024-10-24 version's reign. No activation is recorded under this 2024-10-24 version itself before it was superseded by the 2026-06-03 development successor.

## Key decisions & rationale
35% threshold flags seasons projected worse than ~65% of history, corresponding to roughly a 1-in-3-year severity — validated against a 1998–2022 historical "bad years" consensus list (built from cereal-deficit villages, millet production, food insecurity (EVIAM), and fodder-balance data; refined to a 42-indicator list from January 2023). Dual basis (forecast + SPI backstop) so a missed forecast signal can still be caught by observed deficit — the SPI arm was added to the design in July 2022, and the two-consecutive-month persistence rule was added in July 2023, both as false-alarm-suppression refinements. Two windows align to the agricultural crisis timeline (harvest-saving vs. impact-mitigation). Thresholds are locked to real historical observations (no interpolation) in the repo for interpretability. From January 2023, forecasts are computed only for the area south of the 17th parallel (a dry mask over the desert region, per DMN recommendation).

## Changes from previous version
Update of the 2022 pilot (approved 4 Aug 2022, activated Aug 2022). Per the PDF's introduction, lessons from the 2022 activation were incorporated as: an improved trigger (the SPI observational arm and the two-consecutive-month persistence rule, both added mid-cycle in 2022/2023), streamlined/rationalized activities (e.g. **WFP's** degraded-land rehabilitation / Food-for-Assets activity was cancelled from Window 2 — the doc's "Améliorations par rapport à 2022" cites its development-programme character, the longer site-targeting and training lead times, unresolved land-tenure (*foncier*) questions, and the risk of raising expectations a short project can't sustain), and additional agency co-financing (WFP, FAO, UNICEF, UNHCR). The coordinated AA approach was also extended to floods in Niger (a separate framework, out of scope here). Lineage and per-version differences: see [README](README.md).
