# Bangladesh Flood — 2025-04-25

> The canonical trigger is described in the 2025 framework PDF (`framework_doc`). The repo (`code_ref`) contains the underlying analysis (GloFAS return-period calibration, FFWC station analysis) that derived the trigger but does not implement a live monitoring pipeline.

## Summary

Bangladesh's monsoon flood anticipatory action framework uses a two-step trigger system to release pre-arranged CERF funding ahead of severe flooding in the Jamuna and Padma river basins. A probabilistic GloFAS-based readiness trigger fires at up to 15 days' lead time (Jamuna: maximum 15 days; Padma: 12–15 days); a deterministic FFWC-based action trigger fires at 3–5 days. The framework covers 8 districts and 38 upazilas. Each basin carries its **own** CERF envelope (Jamuna $4.33M, Padma $1.67M), so one basin firing leaves the other still able to trigger (`all_in: false`). The framework as a whole has activated twice — July 2020 and 4 July 2024 (both Jamuna) — but **neither activation was under this version**; see *Historical activations*. This 2025 version is pre-approved for up to $6M from CERF covering the 2025 and 2026 monsoon seasons and, as of September 2026, has not fired.

## Method

The framework combines two forecast systems:
- **GloFAS** (Global Flood Awareness System): a probabilistic ensemble model providing 15-day river discharge forecasts. Calibrated against GloFAS reanalysis to derive 1-in-5-year return period thresholds at specific gauging stations. **v3.1 is the only version the framework doc names**; the live Copernicus EMS service has been v4.x since 26 July 2023, which is the version mismatch recorded in `discrepancies`.
- **FFWC** (Bangladesh Flood Forecasting & Warning Centre): a deterministic operational system providing 5-day water level forecasts at key gauging stations, referenced against government-defined "Danger Levels."
- **RIMES** (Regional Integrated Multi-Hazard Early Warning System): also used alongside GloFAS to confirm sustained/increasing trend for the action trigger.

Monitoring is done manually by an OCHA-led team (led by H.M. Shahid Hassan) who populate two Google Sheets log files (one per basin) twice daily from June onward. When a trigger condition is met, the monitoring team emails the Resident Coordinator and agencies. There is no automated pipeline in the repo.

## Trigger logic

- **Keys off:** GloFAS ensemble discharge probabilities (readiness); FFWC deterministic water level forecasts + GloFAS/RIMES trend confirmation (action).
- **Decision rule:** There are four trigger conditions — two basins (Jamuna, Padma), each with a readiness and an action window:
  - *Readiness (Jamuna):* GloFAS ensemble **more than 50%** likely that discharge at Bahadurabad crosses the 1-in-5-year RP (100,000 m³/s) over 3 consecutive days, at maximum 15-day lead time.
  - *Readiness (Padma):* GloFAS ensemble **more than 50%** likely that discharge at Baruria transit near Goalando crosses the 1-in-5-year RP over 3 consecutive days, at 12–15-day lead time. The doc states no m³/s equivalent for this station.
  - *Action (Jamuna):* FFWC 5-day deterministic forecast crosses government Danger Level + 0.85 m at Bahadurabad, AND GloFAS/RIMES shows sustained or increasing discharge trend at Bahadurabad for at least 3 consecutive days from the day the danger level is forecast to be crossed.
  - *Action (Padma):* FFWC 5-day deterministic forecast (at 3–5 day lead time) crosses 1-in-5-year RP ("Danger Level" + 0.5 m) at Mawa station, AND GloFAS/RIMES shows sustained or increasing discharge trend at Baruria transit for at least 3 consecutive days.
- **Activation structure:** Two independent basin triggers (Jamuna and Padma), each with its own CERF envelope, so either basin can fire without consuming the other's (`all_in: false`). Within each basin, a readiness trigger normally precedes an action trigger, but an action-without-readiness is also valid — in that case CERF disburses 100% of the funds immediately (footnote 11). CERF cash is released at the season's **first** readiness trigger; agencies may spend on readiness activities between readiness and action, and reimburse the unspent remainder if no action trigger follows by the end of the monsoon season. (Note: the doc's readiness/action distinction is a *spend-authorisation* split defined in the per-agency CERF budgets, not a per-window budget in this framework document — the framework's only stated budget axis is the basin.)
- **Calibration:** 1-in-5-year return period derived from GloFAS reanalysis. "Severe" is defined in the doc as an event of ≥1-in-5-year return period, forecast to affect >40% of a population and/or damage >20% of household assets. The Jamuna 1-in-5-year discharge threshold is stated as 100,000 m³/s — but **only in the annex DRAFT readiness email**, not in the Triggers section; no equivalent value is given for Baruria/Goalando. The repo derives the RP value analytically rather than hardcoding it (`utils.get_return_period_function_analytical`, `rp_target = 5`, in analysis/02, 04, 07). FFWC danger levels are government-defined and the framework adds fixed offsets: **+0.5 m at Mawa, which the doc explicitly equates to the 1-in-5-year return period**, and **+0.85 m at Bahadurabad, which the doc does not equate to any return period** — do not read the Jamuna action trigger as RP-calibrated.
- **Authoritative source:** 2025 framework PDF (`framework_doc`) — approved by the Resident Coordinator 7 April 2025, endorsed by the USG/ERC 25 April 2025. The repo contains the calibration analysis, not a live trigger implementation.
- **Operated by:** the OCHA-led forecast monitoring team in Bangladesh (manual, Google Sheets-based). Frontmatter `operated_by` stays `null` because the operator is OCHA, not an external body — the field marks non-OCHA operators (IRI Maproom, INAM/PRISM…).

**Counting rule:** four distinct windows — one readiness + one action window per basin (Jamuna/Bahadurabad, Padma/Baruria–Mawa). Budget note: the CERF envelope is split per **basin**, not per window, so `funding_rows` are keyed by basin and each covers both of that basin's windows.

## Key decisions & rationale

- **Two-step trigger:** The readiness/action two-step design gives agencies a window to pre-position supplies and mobilize staff before the action trigger fires. Readiness at 12–15 days provides operational lead time; action at 3–5 days confirms imminence.
- **1-in-5-year threshold:** Chosen as the severity level where floods "surpass community coping ability" — defined as affecting >40% of a population and/or damaging >20% of household assets. This calibration was conducted against GloFAS reanalysis.
- **GloFAS v3.1 update:** the upgrade from v2.2 doubled the number of successful pre-activations against historic data, reduced false and missed activations, and is what allowed the readiness trigger to move from a 10-day to a 15-day lead time. The doc lists this under "Improvements since **2020**", so it is a 2020→2023 change carried forward, not new in 2025. It also leaves the framework's stated calibration version one major release behind the operational service (v4.x since July 2023) — see `discrepancies`.
- **Padma basin expansion:** added in the **2023** edition (previously Jamuna-only), selected on a combination of forecast skill, flood risk, INFORM vulnerability and implementation capacity. The Padma readiness trigger specifies a 12–15 day range rather than the Jamuna "maximum 15 days"; the doc does not explain the difference, but a station-specific skill analysis is the plausible reason (the technical Padma trigger analysis it cites is a Google Slides deck, not in the repo).
- **FFWC "Danger Level" offset:** triggering on DL + a fixed offset rather than the raw danger level keeps the trigger to severe events. The two offsets are **not** equivalently justified: the doc states DL+0.5 m at Mawa *is* the 1-in-5-year return period, while DL+0.85 m at Bahadurabad is given as a bare offset with no RP equivalence. The doc also notes FFWC has a negative bias (underprediction) at longer lead times and high water levels, which could cause missed action triggers.
- **CERF no-regrets disbursement:** CERF releases funds on a no-regrets basis immediately upon readiness trigger, with readiness-tranche funds refundable if no action trigger follows. This design balances speed of disbursement with fiduciary accountability.
