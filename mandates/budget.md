# The programme budget — OCHA's mandate as planned, reviewed and appropriated each year

**In one paragraph.** Once a year OCHA writes down what it does and Member States approve it, through the regular-budget machinery rather than a humanitarian resolution. The Secretary-General's **proposed programme budget** carries a fascicle for **Section 27, Humanitarian assistance (Programme 23)**: part I is OCHA, part II is UNDRR. Part A of the fascicle — the **programme plan** — opens with a mandate statement ("The mandate is derived from the priorities established in relevant General Assembly resolutions and decisions, including resolution 46/182 …", A/80/6 (Sect. 27) ¶27.I.1), the strategy for the year, the list of **legislative mandates** (¶27.I.10, see [directly-addressed.md §3](directly-addressed.md#3-ochas-own-list--the-legislative-mandates-paragraph-of-the-budget-fascicle)), and the four subprogrammes with their results and deliverables; part B is the post and non-post resources. The plan goes to the **Committee for Programme and Coordination** (CPC, May–June), whose report chapter on Programme 23 the General Assembly endorses in its December "**Programme planning**" resolution; the resources are appropriated in the "**Programme budget for <year>**" resolution. Two facts to keep in view: the regular-budget appropriation for the whole section is small — **$17.8 million for 2026** (80/244 A, Section 27) — against **$365 million of extrabudgetary resources for OCHA alone** (A/80/6 (Sect. 27) ¶27.I.71), so the budget documents describe the mandate far better than they fund it; and in five of the last seven years the CPC reached **no substantive conclusion** on Programme 23 — one year of discussion only, four years in which it simply referred the plan to the Assembly.

## How the chain works

1. **Fascicle (March–April).** The Secretary-General issues `A/<session>/6 (Sect. 27)`. Since the 2020 budget it is annual — the biennial fascicles end with A/72/6 for 2018–2019; the change of budget cycle was decided in resolution 72/266 A, which every programme-planning resolution since recalls in its preamble (not extracted here) — and before that the fascicle covered a biennium, and the section number moved with the budget's structure (25 → 27 → 26 → 27, table below). Part A (programme plan and performance) is submitted "through the Committee for Programme and Coordination", part B (resources) "through the Advisory Committee on Administrative and Budgetary Questions" (A/80/6 (Sect. 27), front-matter footnotes citing 77/267 ¶10).
2. **CPC (May–June).** The Committee — "the main subsidiary organ of the General Assembly and the Economic and Social Council for planning, programming and coordination" (80/233 OP1) — considers Programme 23 in one meeting, records a "Discussion" and, if it agrees, "Conclusions and recommendations". Where it cannot agree, 79/247 and 80/233 provide that the plenary or the relevant Main Committee considers the programme at the start of the session instead (80/233 OP7).
3. **Programme planning resolution (December).** The Assembly "endorses the conclusions and recommendations of the Committee for Programme and Coordination on the proposed programme plan" (80/233 OP9), which is how the CPC's text on Programme 23 — when there is one — becomes an Assembly decision.
4. **Programme budget resolution (December).** Resolution A appropriates by section: "27 Humanitarian assistance" is one line of Part VI (80/244 A, ¶1).

The Fifth Committee, not the humanitarian negotiators of the December omnibus, handles steps 3 and 4; the two tracks meet only in the fascicle's list of legislative mandates.

**What the CPC said.** 2019 (A/74/16): discussion only, no conclusions (¶384–396; a delegation objected that the plan's wording "must be based on resolutions of the General Assembly and the Economic and Social Council", ¶395). 2020: "Owing to a shortage of time, the Committee recommended that the General Assembly review … the programme plan of programme 23" (A/75/16 ¶526). 2021: referred to the Assembly again (A/76/16 ¶486). 2022: the Committee "commended the work carried out by the Office" and recommended that the Assembly encourage it "to continue to ensure the timely, coherent, coordinated and principled response of the international community to natural disasters and complex emergencies" (A/77/16 ¶620–621). 2023: substantive again — recommended that the Assembly ask the Secretary-General, as Chair of CEB, to consolidate system capacities for "safe and unhindered humanitarian access", and ask OCHA to strengthen partnerships with the Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and humanitarian NGOs (A/78/16 ¶748–750). 2024 and 2025: referred to "the plenary or the relevant Main Committee or Main Committees" under the new rule (A/79/16 ¶611; A/80/16 ¶493). The 2025 discussion is worth reading for the team: delegations asked about **data quality in humanitarian reporting** and "how the Office ensured impartiality and accuracy when information originated from local sources affiliated with non-State actors" (A/80/16 ¶476), questioned the use of ReliefWeb "as a source of 'reliable' information" (¶481), and several "expressed support for anticipatory action and innovative financing" (¶475).

**The four subprogrammes** (A/80/6 (Sect. 27), contents and ¶27.I.11 ff.): 1 Policy and analysis; 2 Coordination of humanitarian action and emergency response (the bulk of the extrabudgetary resources, $283 million in 2026); 3 Emergency support services; 4 Humanitarian emergency information and advocacy. The deliverables tables under each (27.I.2 … 27.I.12) are the one place where OCHA states yearly counts of what it produces — appeals, situation reports, UNDAC deployments — in a document Member States have reviewed.

## 3. What this means for the team's work

- **When someone asks what OCHA "officially" does this year,** the answer is in the fascicle's part A, not on the website: the mandate statement (¶27.I.1), the strategy (¶27.I.3 — in 2026 it includes "strengthen anticipatory approaches and early warning early action systems" and "advancing innovative and anticipatory financing mechanisms"), the legislative mandates list, and the subprogramme results. It is the one document in which OCHA itself connects the annual resolutions to its own work plan.
- **The regular budget is not where OCHA's money is.** Quoting the $17.8 million appropriation as "OCHA's budget" is wrong by a factor of twenty; the fascicle's extrabudgetary tables are the comparable figure, and even they exclude CERF and the CBPFs.
- **The CPC chapter is the only intergovernmental review of OCHA's programme plan**, and it is where Member States have raised the reliability of OCHA's information products (2025). Anything we build that feeds OCHA's reporting should expect that question.
