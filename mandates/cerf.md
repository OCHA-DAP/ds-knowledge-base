# CERF — mandate and operating rules

**In one paragraph.** CERF is a General Assembly creation run by the Secretariat. **Resolution 46/182 (1991)** asked the Secretary-General to establish "under his authority a central emergency *revolving* fund as a cash-flow mechanism" of **$50 million**, lending to UN agencies against expected appeal contributions (annex ¶22–26), managed by the ERC (¶35(e)). **Resolution 60/124 (15 December 2005)** "decide[d] to upgrade" it into the Central Emergency *Response* Fund by adding a **grant element** with three objectives — *promote early action and response to reduce loss of life; enhance response to time-critical requirements; strengthen core elements of humanitarian response in underfunded crises* (¶15) — still operating under 46/182's principles (¶16), with the Assembly keeping policy guidance (¶17) and an independent Advisory Group (¶18). Later resolutions cut the loan element to **$30 million** (66/119 ¶25) and set the **$1 billion annual funding target** (71/127 ¶50; renewed with a 2025 target in 79/140 ¶61). The rules by which the ERC runs the Fund are a **Secretary-General's bulletin**, currently **ST/SGB/2020/5** (1 January 2020), which fixes eligibility, the loan and grant terms, the Rapid Response / Underfunded Emergencies split, implementation periods and reporting.

Authority levels and how to read the citations: [README.md](README.md). Extracts: [`raw/mandates/`](../raw/mandates/).

## 1. The Revolving Fund — 46/182 annex ¶21–26 (1991)

Section IV(a) of the annex, "Contingency funding arrangements": agencies should keep their own reserves (¶21), but "there is a need for a complementary central funding mechanism to ensure the provision of adequate resources for use in the **initial phase** of emergencies that require a system-wide response" (¶22). Hence ¶23: "the Secretary-General should establish under his authority a central emergency revolving fund as a **cash-flow mechanism** to ensure the rapid and coordinated response of the organizations of the system." ¶24 sets the size — "**50 million United States dollars**", financed by voluntary contributions "on an assured, broad-based and additional basis", with a donor meeting in the first quarter of 1992. ¶25 is the loan logic: resources "advanced to the operational organizations of the system on the understanding that they would **reimburse** the fund in the first instance from the voluntary contributions received in response to consolidated appeals." ¶26: review after two years. The ERC manages the fund (¶35(e)) and reports on it annually through ECOSOC to the Assembly (¶35(i)); ST/SGB/1999/8 §3.2(h) lists "Manages the Central Emergency Revolving Fund" among the USG/ERC's functions ([ocha.md](ocha.md) §3).

## 2. The Response Fund — 60/124 ¶15–22 (2005)

Acting on the Secretary-General's report on *the improvement of the Central Emergency Revolving Fund* (A/60/432, cited in ST/SGB/2020/5 §1.2), the Assembly on 15 December 2005:

- **¶15 — upgrades** "the current Central Emergency Revolving Fund into the Central Emergency Response Fund by including a **grant element** based on voluntary contributions, to be replenished at regular intervals, so as to ensure a more predictable and timely response to humanitarian emergencies, with the objectives of **promoting early action and response to reduce loss of life, enhancing response to time-critical requirements and strengthening core elements of humanitarian response in underfunded crises**, based on demonstrable needs and on priorities identified in consultation with the affected State as appropriate". *The resolution names no dollar figure*: the $450 million grant target and $500 million total of the 2005–06 design are in the Secretary-General's proposal and in the first bulletin (ST/SGB/2006/10 §1.3: "$500 million funded from voluntary contributions … an overall target of $450 million to provide grants"), not in the Assembly's text.
- **¶16** — the Fund "will continue to operate in accordance with resolution 46/182 and the guiding principles contained in the annex thereto".
- **¶17** — the Assembly "affirms its role to provide overall policy guidance on the use of the Fund" and encourages ECOSOC to discuss its implementation.
- **¶18** — "takes note that an **advisory group** will be established, as an independent body, to provide advice to the Secretary-General on the use and impact of the Fund", and that the IASC will discuss its use and impact.
- **¶19** — the Secretary-General to make the managerial arrangements and "appropriate reporting and accountability mechanisms"; **¶20** — contributions "should be additional to current commitments to humanitarian programming"; **¶21** — detailed reporting to the Assembly and ECOSOC; **¶22** — an independent review after two years (the two-year evaluation, then the five-year evaluation of 2011).

## 3. The operating rules — ST/SGB/2020/5 (in force since 1 January 2020)

Three bulletins have carried the Fund's rules: **ST/SGB/2006/10** (10 October 2006), **ST/SGB/2010/5** (23 April 2010) and **ST/SGB/2020/5** (1 January 2020), each abolishing its predecessor (2010/5 §7.2 abolishes 2006/10; 2020/5 §7.2 abolishes 2010/5). The first of them had in turn abolished ST/SGB/251, the bulletin under which the Revolving Fund had been run (2006/10 §7.2). The current one is promulgated "for the purposes of establishing a regime for the administration and management of the loan and grant components of the Central Emergency Response Fund, established in accordance with General Assembly resolutions 46/182, 60/124, 66/119 and 71/127" (preamble). What it fixes:

| Topic | Rule (section) |
|---|---|
| **Legal form** | One general trust fund, "the Central Emergency Response Fund", replacing the Revolving Fund; loan and grant elements administered separately (§1.3 + fn 1); governed by the UN Financial Regulations and Rules (§1.4) |
| **Size** | Loan element "up to **$30 million**" (66/119); grant element "an annual target of **$970 million**" (71/127) — together the $1 billion (§1.3) |
| **Who decides** | The USG for Humanitarian Affairs and ERC ("the Coordinator") has "overall responsibility for funding decisions and programmatic management" (§1.5), supported by a secretariat and other OCHA units, consulting the IASC and Resident/Humanitarian Coordinators (§1.6) |
| **Eligibility (§2)** | "The United Nations, its funds, programmes and specialized agencies, as well as the International Organization for Migration" — i.e. **no direct NGO or government access**; OCHA itself may take loans but not grants |
| **Loan element (§3)** | Cash-flow mechanism for rapid, coordinated response (§3.1); exchange of letters; repayment "as a first charge on contributions received" and "within one year" unless the Coordinator agrees otherwise (§3.3); extraordinary advances allowed in urgent cases, to be regularised within 30 days (§3.4); recovery measures after one year, best effort within two (§3.7–3.8). Interest above $30M flows to the grant element (§3.1) |
| **Grant element objectives (§4.1)** | (a) promote early action and response to reduce loss of life; (b) enhance response to time-critical requirements based on demonstrable needs; (c) strengthen core elements of humanitarian response in underfunded crises — 60/124 ¶15 verbatim |
| **Rapid Response (§4.2)** | "Primarily … immediate funding to ensure a rapid response to humanitarian, life-saving needs due to sudden-onset emergencies, time-critical crises or rapid deterioration of existing crises"; **up to two thirds** of the grant element "shall normally be utilized" for it |
| **Underfunded Emergencies (§4.3–4.5)** | The remainder, for "core emergency humanitarian needs in chronically underfunded emergencies"; access limited to contexts **selected by the Coordinator** after reviewing needs, funding status and consulting the IASC, agencies, RC/HCs and the affected State; allocations "normally … **twice a year**" |
| **Who applies (§4.6–4.9)** | RC/HCs, with the humanitarian country team, propose priorities from recent needs assessments and the appeal; requests are "primarily **field-driven**" and address "what in the context of that crisis constitutes humanitarian, life-saving needs"; agencies may apply directly only exceptionally (e.g. transboundary crises) |
| **Not a substitute (§4.10)** | Grants "will not be intended to replace the existing humanitarian appeal mechanisms, which shall remain the primary humanitarian funding tool"; no duplicate funding |
| **Pre-request commitments (§4.13)** | "In exceptional circumstances, the Coordinator may commit funds for an emergency to address urgent needs before the receipt of a grant request" |
| **Implementation periods** | Rapid Response grants "normally … implemented within **six months**" of disbursement (§4.15); Underfunded grants within **nine months** (§4.16); both adjustable by the Coordinator; cancellation / no-cost-extension handling (§4.17) |
| **Replenishment (§4.18)** | The Secretary-General may convene an annual pledging conference |
| **Reporting (§5.1–5.3)** | Agencies: certified financial report per grant as at 31 December by 15 February; annual narrative report at HQ level plus field inputs; RC/HCs oversee monitoring and submit a narrative report; the Secretary-General reports to the Assembly under 46/182, 60/124 "and applicable subsequent resolutions" |
| **Return of funds, suspension (§5.4–5.5)** | Unspent balances returned; the Coordinator may suspend an agency that fails to return funds or report |
| **Evaluation, PSEA (§5.6–5.10)** | Independent evaluations as needed; participation in the performance and accountability framework; zero tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse; the public CERF website as an accountability tool |
| **Advisory Group (§6)** | "Up to **19 independent experts** who broadly represent the humanitarian community", appointed by the Secretary-General, advising through the Coordinator "on the implementation of the Fund's mandate as a whole, including on the speed and appropriateness of its allocations" |

## 4. Resolutions since 2005 — what changed

- **66/119 (15 December 2011) ¶25–26.** Following the five-year evaluation, "decides to reduce the size of the loan element of the Fund to **30 million United States dollars**" with the balance and interest moved to the grant element; and authorises, "in exceptional circumstances and on a time-bound basis", use of the loan element "to enhance … rapid response coordination where insufficient capacity exists at the field level".
- **71/127 (8 December 2016) ¶49–50.** Welcomes the Fund's achievements and, "welcoming … the call by the Secretary-General to increase the level of the Fund to 1 billion United States dollars by 2018" (preamble), calls on Member States and the private sector "to achieve an **annual funding level of 1 billion United States dollars by 2018**" and to "reinforce and strengthen the Fund as **the global emergency response fund**". The bulletin translates this into $30M loan + $970M grant (§1.3).
- **Every December since**, the annual coordination resolution repeats the two-paragraph CERF block (achievements + the $1 billion call) and adds detail. **79/140 (9 December 2024) ¶60–62**: timely disbursement to implementing partners; the $1 billion call now "including, where appropriate, through multi-year funding" and "accelerated efforts to achieve the 1 billion dollar annual funding level **by 2025**, marking the twentieth anniversary of the Fund"; plus continued support to CERF and the country-based and regional pooled funds. The December 2025 draft (A/80/L.25 ¶62–64) carries this forward. <!-- TODO: replace with the adopted A/RES/80 text when its symbol is confirmed -->
- **Oversight record.** The Secretary-General's annual reports on the Fund to the Assembly run from A/61/85 (2005) to A/80/384 (2024); CERF lists them all at <https://cerf.un.org/about-us/who-we-are/secretary-general-reports-and-bulletins>. They are the authoritative account of what was allocated and why in a given year, and the place where anticipatory action appears in official UN documentation.

## 5. Anticipatory action — where it sits in this layer

**No General Assembly resolution and no bulletin names an anticipatory action window, trigger, or framework.** The mandate-level hooks are:

- the grant element's first objective, **"promote early action and response to reduce loss of life"** (60/124 ¶15; ST/SGB/2020/5 §4.1(a)) — in practice the objective CERF's anticipatory allocations are made under, though neither document says so (see the TODO below);
- the Coordinator's power to commit funds **before a request** in exceptional circumstances (§4.13) and the agencies' six-month Rapid Response implementation period (§4.15);
- the Assembly's encouragement, since 73/139 (2018), of "innovative and anticipatory mechanisms and approaches, such as forecast-based financing", and since 76/124 (2021) to "scale up anticipatory approaches, early warning early action systems, forecasting … and improve predictive and risk data analytics" ([ocha.md](ocha.md) §5).

## 6. What this means for the team's work

- **Eligible recipients are UN agencies and IOM only (§2).** Framework budgets that name NGO or Red Cross implementers are describing agency sub-grants or a parallel pooled fund (e.g. a country-based pooled fund), not CERF grants — which is why several framework documents pair a CERF allocation with a CBPF one.
- **Windows and the two-thirds rule (§4.2–4.3).** Anticipatory frameworks draw on the grant element under objective (a); the Rapid Response / Underfunded split is a bulletin rule about the element as a whole, not a cap on anticipatory action. When a framework page says "window", it is using CERF's operational vocabulary, not the bulletin's.
- **Implementation clocks (§4.15–4.16)** are what framework documents mean by "funds to be spent within six months of disbursement".
- **Our data.** The allocation record we mirror ([infrastructure/datasets/cerf-onegms.md](../infrastructure/datasets/cerf-onegms.md), [pipelines/cerf-supplement.md](../pipelines/cerf-supplement.md)) is the OneGMS implementation of the §5 reporting chain; its application/project grain mirrors the exchange-of-letters-per-agency structure of §4.11.
- **The $1 billion is a *funding level* target set by the Assembly, repeated yearly** — not a legal ceiling; the bulletin's $970M grant figure derives from it (§1.3).
