# The annual resolutions — the three series that renew OCHA's mandate

**In one paragraph.** Resolution 46/182 is the frame; what OCHA is *currently* mandated to do is written each year into three resolutions that Member States negotiate on the basis of the Secretary-General's report: the **General Assembly's omnibus** "Strengthening of the coordination of emergency humanitarian assistance of the United Nations" (every December since 1991), the **ECOSOC resolution** of the same title adopted at the Council's humanitarian affairs segment (every summer, 2002–2024, with precursors in 1995–1996), and the General Assembly's **natural disasters resolution** "International cooperation on humanitarian assistance in the field of natural disasters, from relief to development" (every session since 1999). Together they are the living text of the mandate: largely cumulative, renumbered every year, and the place where new language — protection, access, localisation, climate, data, anticipatory action — first becomes something Member States have agreed to. **The record is no longer unbroken.** The General Assembly omnibus was **withdrawn on 10 December 2025** (draft A/80/L.25) after thirty-three consecutive consensus adoptions, the natural disasters and safety-of-personnel texts went to recorded votes the same day, and ECOSOC adopted **no humanitarian resolution in 2020, 2025 or, so far, 2026**. The latest adopted texts are therefore **79/140 (9 December 2024)**, **80/113 (10 December 2025)** and **ECOSOC 2024/8 (27 June 2024)**. This page is the registry of all three series (generated from [registry.yml](registry.yml)), what each gap in the record rests on, and where the themes our work depends on first entered the text. The OCHA page ([ocha.md](ocha.md)) reads the mandate itself; this page is about the documents that carry it.

## How the three series fit together

**Why there are three.** 46/182 ¶35(i) asked the ERC to report annually to the General Assembly *through* the Economic and Social Council, so from the start the mandate had two intergovernmental tracks. The Council's track became a formal **humanitarian affairs segment** by General Assembly decision in the 1997 reform (52/12 B OP18: "Decides to establish a humanitarian affairs segment of the Economic and Social Council, with effect from 1998"), after two Council resolutions of the mid-1990s had already used the title — 1995/56, which commissioned the review of the humanitarian system that fed the 1997 reform (OP1: "a comprehensive and analytical report … for a review and strengthening of all aspects of the capacity of the United Nations system for humanitarian assistance"), and 1996/33, which set that report's timetable. The segment's first years produced *agreed conclusions* (decisions 1998/1 and 1999/1, cited throughout the OCHA Reference Guide) rather than resolutions; the resolution form begins with 2002/32. The **natural disasters** series is the Assembly's second humanitarian text, adopted under the same agenda item and usually in the same plenary meeting as the omnibus (always since the seventieth session — compare the meeting numbers in the two tables; in the 54th, 58th, 59th, 64th–67th and 69th sessions it was adopted on a different day, twice in the following January); it began in 1999 (54/233) when the International Decade for Natural Disaster Reduction ended.

**The same procedure every year.** All three are adopted without reference to a Main Committee (the Assembly ones) or in the Council's plenary, on a draft tabled by a facilitator (the `Draft` column), traditionally **without a vote**. The Secretary-General's report of the same title (symbol `A/<session>/<n>–E/<year>/<n>`, issued for the summer segment) is the common input to the ECOSOC text and the December omnibus. The reports are not extracted here; they are level-3 documents under the [README's authority rule](README.md#how-to-read-these-pages).

**Cite the year.** Each series renumbers its operative paragraphs every year, the omnibus grew from 10 operative paragraphs (47/168) to 90 (79/140), and much of the text is carried forward with small changes. A citation is therefore always `symbol OP<n>` (or `PP<n>` for the preamble — the Reference Guide's convention, adopted here): `79/140 OP59`, not "the humanitarian resolution says". The title order flipped once: 46/182–48/57 say "humanitarian emergency assistance"; from 49/139 A it is "emergency humanitarian assistance".

**Authority.** All three are Member-State decisions and sit at the top of the [authority ladder](README.md#how-to-read-these-pages). The 2009 Reference Guide states the Secretariat's reading plainly: "all legislative decisions taken in each of the three intergovernmental bodies are equally binding on the UN Secretariat and recommendatory on Member States, with the notable exception of decisions of the Security Council related to the maintenance of international peace and security that are obligatory for Member States" (2009 ed., Preface, p. 1).

**2025: withdrawn, not adopted.** The 60th plenary meeting of 10 December 2025 took up draft A/80/L.25 together with five amendments: four from the United States (A/80/L.30–L.33 — among them L.32, which would have replaced "sexual and reproductive" with "emergency" in the draft's OP69, and amendments to the climate-change language of PP4 and PP15) and one from the Russian Federation (A/80/L.36, inserting a paragraph on unilateral coercive measures); the amendment documents are in ODS under those symbols. The facilitator urged the sponsors of the amendments to withdraw them and, when they did not, **withdrew the draft** "to protect the interests of the hundreds of millions for whom it was tabled" (press release GA/12742, which also records that the text "has now enjoyed an impressive 33 years of consensus"). No A/RES/80 symbol exists for it; the UN's machine-readable list of eightieth-session resolutions has none under this title. The verbatim record A/80/PV.60 was not in the Official Document System on 2026-10-09; when it is, cite it in place of the press release. <!-- TODO: replace the GA/12742 citation with A/80/PV.60 when ODS publishes it, and record what the eighty-first session does with the omnibus (a revived L.25, a new draft, or nothing). --> The two humanitarian texts that *were* adopted that day, 80/112 (safety and security of humanitarian personnel) and 80/113 (natural disasters, below), both went to **recorded votes** rather than the customary adoption without a vote. The practical consequence for the team is that **79/140 of 9 December 2024 remains the latest General Assembly text of OCHA's mandate**, and the anticipatory-action paragraphs cited on [ocha.md §5](ocha.md#5-the-living-mandate--the-annual-coordination-resolution) stand as adopted there. The withdrawn draft is kept as an extract because it shows what had been negotiated (its OP42–43 and OP61 carry the anticipatory-action language forward unchanged).

## 2. General Assembly — "International cooperation on humanitarian assistance in the field of natural disasters, from relief to development"

The disaster-side text, adopted alongside the omnibus. **The gaps.** ECOSOC's series is the one that has lapsed most often. In **2020** the segment was held virtually (9–11 June) and a draft was tabled (E/2020/L.13 of 4 June 2020, kept as an extract), but no resolution was adopted: the official records of the 2020 session (E/2020/99) and of the 2021 session (E/2021/99, which would have captured a later adoption in 2020) contain no resolution under this title, and the first one after 2019/14 is 2021/17 of 25 June 2021. In **2025** the segment met in Geneva from 18 to 20 June (decision 2025/304 set its theme) and the official records (E/2025/99) carry no humanitarian resolution; neither does the Council's resolutions index. For **2026** the Council's index lists 22 resolutions to 4 August 2026, none under this title (checked 2026-10-09), so the latest ECOSOC text is **2024/8 of 27 June 2024**. The statement that the series is "annual" is therefore true of its design and of 2002–2019, not of the record since. <!-- TODO: when E/2026/99 is issued, confirm 2026 and update the registry; if a 2025 or 2026 text was adopted as a decision rather than a resolution, record that instead. -->

## 4. Where the team's themes entered the text

First appearance of each phrase in each series (46/182 counted as the first of the omnibus series), found by text search over the extracts and read in place; the paragraph is the one that contains the phrase. Earlier resolutions may carry the idea in other words, so these are first *uses of the phrase*, not first mentions of the idea. Numbers are operative paragraphs unless marked PP; for 46/182 they are annex paragraphs.

| Theme | GA omnibus (first) | GA natural disasters (first) | ECOSOC (first) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **early warning** | 46/182 ¶19 (1991): "the systematic pooling, analysis and dissemination of early-warning information on natural disasters and other emergencies"; 48/57 OP7 (1993) then asks for an emergency information system in DHA "to provide early warning of a crisis" | 54/233 OP4 (1999): "prevention, preparedness and early warning systems" | 2003/5 OP17: national and regional capacity "in early warning and monitoring of natural hazards" |

Three things the table shows that matter for how we describe our work: the Assembly's first "anticipatory" sentence is in the **disaster** resolution, four years before the omnibus; **ECOSOC had "forecast-based financing" (2017/14) a year before the Assembly (73/139)**, consistent with the summer text feeding the December one; and the phrase "early action" entered the General Assembly's humanitarian vocabulary as a **CERF objective** (60/124 OP15), which is the textual hook [cerf.md §5](cerf.md#5-anticipatory-action--what-the-ga-and-bulletin-layer-does-and-does-not-say) relies on.

## 5. The OCHA Reference Guide

OCHA's Policy Development and Studies Branch has three times compiled the operative language of these resolutions by theme — "Normative developments on the coordination of humanitarian assistance in the General Assembly, the Economic and Social Council (and the Security Council) since the adoption of General Assembly resolution 46/182". It is the Secretariat's own index to the series above: each page pairs a paraphrase with the `symbol, OP/PP` it comes from, in fifteen (2009) or sixteen (2015) thematic chapters that follow the structure of 46/182. Two limits are stated in its preface and matter for the [directly-addressed page](directly-addressed.md): it "does not include intergovernmental decisions adopted for specific humanitarian emergencies or disasters", and only the first edition covered the Security Council.

## 6. Refreshing this page

The series grow by at most one resolution each per year. To add one: fetch the PDF from the ODS symbol endpoint (`https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=<url-encoded symbol>&l=E&t=pdf`), run `pdftotext -layout`, strip trailing whitespace, save it as `raw/mandates/<SYMBOL>.txt`, take the adoption date and meeting from the plenary line at the end of the text (never from the `Distr.` header or an index — the ECOSOC website's dates are issuance dates and disagree with the plenary lines in several years), add a row to [registry.yml](registry.yml), and run `scripts/mandates_registry.py`. The machine-readable UN resolution sets at <https://github.com/UNxml> (General Assembly from the 74th session, ECOSOC from 2021, Security Council from 2000) are the quickest way to confirm a symbol and to grep for a phrase across a whole session. Recent General Assembly sessions are also listed at <https://research.un.org/en/docs/ga/quick/regular/><session> (through the 76th) and the ECOSOC year pages at <https://ecosoc.un.org/en/documents/resolutions/year/><year>.
