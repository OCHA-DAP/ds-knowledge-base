# OCHA and the Emergency Relief Coordinator — mandate

**In one paragraph.** OCHA has no founding resolution of its own. The General Assembly, in **resolution 46/182 of 19 December 1991**, set out guiding principles for humanitarian assistance and asked the Secretary-General to designate a high-level official — the **Emergency Relief Coordinator (ERC)** — with nine listed responsibilities, "supported by a secretariat" (¶34–36), and created the **Inter-Agency Standing Committee**, the **consolidated appeal** and a **central emergency revolving fund** around that official (¶31, ¶38, ¶21–26). The Secretary-General first built that secretariat as the Department of Humanitarian Affairs (1992); the **1997 reform programme (A/51/950 ¶184–191)** replaced it with a smaller Office focused on three core functions, and the **1999 bulletin ST/SGB/1999/8** gave that Office — OCHA — its organisation and functions. The mandate is **renewed and extended every December** by a resolution titled "Strengthening of the coordination of emergency humanitarian assistance of the United Nations" (46/182 ¶35(i) made the reporting annual), the latest adopted text being **79/140 of 9 December 2024** — the December 2025 draft (A/80/L.25) was withdrawn before adoption, the first break in the series since 1991 ([resolutions.md](resolutions.md)). Four humanitarian principles frame all of it: humanity, neutrality and impartiality from 46/182 ¶2, with **independence** added by **58/114 (2003)**.

Authority levels and how to read the citations: [README.md](README.md). Extracts: [`raw/mandates/`](../raw/mandates/).

## 1. Resolution 46/182 (1991) — the framework

The resolution itself is two operative paragraphs: it *adopts the annex* and asks for a report at the next session (op. ¶1–2). Everything of substance is in the **annex**, 42 paragraphs in seven sections. What each section does, with the paragraphs we cite most:

| Annex section | What it establishes | Paragraphs worth knowing |
|---|---|---|
| **I. Guiding principles** | The framework for all UN humanitarian assistance | ¶2 humanity, neutrality, impartiality · ¶3 sovereignty; assistance "with the consent of the affected country and in principle on the basis of an appeal" · ¶4 the affected State "has the primary role" · ¶8 "special attention … to disaster prevention and preparedness" · ¶9 relief–rehabilitation–development continuum · ¶11 humanitarian contributions "not to the detriment of" development resources · ¶12 the UN's "central and unique role" in leadership and coordination |
| **II. Prevention** | Capacity for prevention and mitigation; links to the International Decade for Natural Disaster Reduction | ¶13–17 |
| **III. Preparedness** | Capacity-building; **early warning** | ¶19 the UN should intensify "the systematic pooling, analysis and dissemination of early-warning information on natural disasters and other emergencies" · ¶20 early-warning information "made available in an unrestricted and timely manner" to all interested Governments, with capacity support to disaster-prone countries |
| **IV. Stand-by capacity** | (a) **contingency funding** — the revolving fund · (b) a central register of stand-by personnel, supplies and services; emergency rules for fast disbursement, procurement and recruitment | ¶22–26 the fund (see [cerf.md](cerf.md) §1) · ¶27–30 register and emergency procedures |
| **V. Consolidated appeals** | An initial inter-agency appeal "within the shortest possible time and in any event not longer than one week", updated within four weeks for prolonged emergencies | ¶31–32 |
| **VI. Coordination, cooperation and leadership** | The Secretary-General's leadership; **the ERC**; the ERC's secretariat; **the IASC**; the resident coordinator at country level | ¶33–39 (below) |
| **VII. Continuum** | Relief in ways "supportive of recovery and long-term development" | ¶40–42 |

### The Emergency Relief Coordinator (¶33–37)

- **Designation (¶34).** A "high-level official (emergency relief coordinator)" designated by the Secretary-General, "to work closely with and with direct access to him", **combining** the functions previously carried out by the Secretary-General's representatives for major and complex emergencies and by the **UN Disaster Relief Coordinator** (UNDRO). Designation was conditional on the fund's resources (¶24) being provided.
- **Nine responsibilities (¶35), "under the aegis of the General Assembly and working under the direction of the Secretary-General":** (a) processing Member States' requests for emergency assistance requiring a coordinated response; (b) **maintaining an overview of all emergencies "through, inter alia, the systematic pooling and analysis of early-warning information"** (¶19); (c) organising joint inter-agency needs-assessment missions and preparing the consolidated appeal, followed by periodic situation reports; (d) facilitating access, "including through negotiation if needed" — relief corridors, days and zones of tranquillity; (e) **managing the central emergency revolving fund** and assisting resource mobilisation; (f) central focal point with Governments and organisations, including as **Chairman of the IASC**; (g) **providing consolidated information, including early warning**, to Governments and authorities, "particularly affected and disaster-prone countries"; (h) promoting the transition from relief to rehabilitation; (i) **an annual report** to the Secretary-General on coordination, including on the fund, **submitted to the General Assembly through the Economic and Social Council** — the origin of today's annual resolution cycle.
- **The secretariat (¶36)** is to be "based on a strengthened Office of the United Nations Disaster Relief Coordinator and the consolidation of existing offices that deal with complex emergencies", supplementable by seconded staff; the ERC works with the UN system, the ICRC, the League (now Federation) of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies, IOM and NGOs, and "provide[s] leadership to the resident coordinators" on humanitarian matters.

### The IASC and country-level coordination (¶38–39)

¶38 establishes the **Inter-Agency Standing Committee** under the ERC's chairmanship, with all operational organisations participating and a **standing invitation** to the ICRC, the Red Cross/Red Crescent federation and IOM; NGOs may be invited ad hoc. ¶39 has the **resident coordinator** "normally coordinate the humanitarian assistance of the United Nations system at the country level" and chair an emergency operations group — the root of the Resident/Humanitarian Coordinator role the CERF bulletin later relies on ([cerf.md](cerf.md) §3).

## 2. From DHA to OCHA — the 1997 reform (A/51/950 ¶184–191)

The Secretary-General's report *Renewing the United Nations: a programme for reform* (14 July 1997) records that he had "established the Department of Humanitarian Affairs within the Secretariat in 1992 to support the Emergency Relief Coordinator" (¶184), and found that DHA's ad hoc operational activities had "diluted the importance of the Emergency Relief Coordinator's core functions" (¶185). It therefore:

- **Fixed the ERC's three core functions (¶186):** (a) **policy development and coordination** in support of the Secretary-General, including issues that fall between agency mandates such as internally displaced persons; (b) **advocacy** of humanitarian issues with political organs, "notably the Security Council"; (c) **coordination of humanitarian emergency response** by ensuring a response mechanism is established, through IASC consultations, on the ground.
- **Moved the operational functions out (¶187):** coordination of natural-disaster *relief* stays with the ERC; disaster **prevention, mitigation and preparedness** go to UNDP (they "relate to national capabilities"); demining to DPKO; the Iraq Programme to a special unit.
- **Replaced DHA with an "Office of the Emergency Relief Coordinator" (¶188)** — "relatively small in size and well-focused on its core activities", with the ERC as "the Secretary-General's main adviser on humanitarian issues" and direct access to him "as Assembly resolution 46/182 stipulates". That Office is OCHA: the 1999 organisation bulletin names 46/182 and this report as the two sources of the Office's functions (ST/SGB/1999/8, fn 1; §3 below). ¶189's recommendation to rename the ERC "United Nations Humanitarian Assistance Coordinator" was **not** taken up: the 1999 bulletin and every later resolution keep "Emergency Relief Coordinator".
- **Strengthened the IASC** with a steering committee of UNHCR, WFP, UNICEF, UNDP, the ICRC and an NGO representative (¶190), and widened the consolidated appeal to rehabilitation and human-rights field operations (¶191).

## 3. OCHA's organisation and functions — ST/SGB/1999/8 (22 June 1999)

The Secretary-General's bulletin on the *Organization of the Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs* is the document that defines **OCHA** as an entity. Its footnote 1 states the lineage in one sentence: the Office "supports the Under-Secretary-General, the United Nations Emergency Relief Coordinator, in carrying out his or her functions, pursuant to General Assembly resolution 46/182 and the report of the Secretary-General entitled 'Renewing the United Nations: a programme for reform' (A/51/950)".

- **OCHA's functions (§2.1):** (a) assists the Secretary-General in coordinating humanitarian emergencies "that require a system-wide response, by virtue of their magnitude or complexity"; (b) works closely with IASC members and relevant intergovernmental and non-governmental organisations; (c) ensures coordination between the humanitarian, political and peacekeeping dimensions of emergencies; (d) provides substantive support to the General Assembly, the Security Council and ECOSOC on humanitarian issues; (e) seeks to strengthen humanitarian response capabilities for natural disasters, working with the International Decade for Natural Disaster Reduction partners.
- **Headed by the Under-Secretary-General for Humanitarian Affairs, who is the ERC (§2.3)**, accountable to the Secretary-General (§3.1) and his "main adviser on humanitarian issues" (§3.2). The USG's functions (§3.2 a–h) include leadership of emergency humanitarian coordination on the Secretary-General's behalf, chairing the IASC and the Executive Committee on Humanitarian Affairs, guidance to humanitarian coordinators in the field, advocacy for humanitarian law and principles, resource mobilisation, missions to emergencies — and **(h) "Manages the Central Emergency Revolving Fund"**. Footnote 2 restates the three core functions of A/51/950 ¶186.
- **Structure (§4–§15):** Office of the USG and a Deputy ERC in New York; an Assistant ERC who directs OCHA Geneva (§6); an Executive Office that administers trust funds and the revolving fund (§7.2); then the substantive divisions and branches of the day.

## 4. The principles — and the fourth one (58/114, 2003)

46/182 ¶2 names **three** principles: "humanity, neutrality and impartiality". Resolution **58/114 of 17 December 2003** adds the fourth in its preamble: "*Recognizing* that **independence**, meaning the autonomy of humanitarian objectives from the political, economic, military or other objectives that any actor may hold with regard to areas where humanitarian action is being implemented, is also an important guiding principle for the provision of humanitarian assistance". Every annual resolution since then reaffirms all four in its preamble (e.g. 66/119 preambular ¶2: "the principles of neutrality, humanity, impartiality and independence"), and the CERF resolutions bind the Fund to "resolution 46/182 and the guiding principles contained in the annex thereto" (60/124 ¶16).

## 5. The living mandate — the annual resolutions

Because 46/182 ¶35(i) made the ERC's report annual and routed it through ECOSOC, the mandate has standing intergovernmental tracks, and since 1999 there are three texts a year: the **General Assembly's December omnibus** "Strengthening of the coordination of emergency humanitarian assistance of the United Nations", the **ECOSOC resolution** of the same title adopted at the Council's humanitarian affairs segment each summer (created by 52/12 B OP18, 1997), and the Assembly's **natural disasters resolution** "International cooperation on humanitarian assistance in the field of natural disasters, from relief to development", adopted alongside it each December. All three are registered, year by year, on [resolutions.md](resolutions.md), which also tracks where each theme the team works with first entered each series. The omnibus is long (79/140 runs to 90 operative paragraphs), largely cumulative, and **renumbers its operative paragraphs every year**, so cite the symbol. Three facts about the record, all from the documents: the omnibus was **not adopted in December 2025** — draft A/80/L.25 was withdrawn by its sponsor at the 60th plenary meeting on 10 December 2025 after amendments were tabled, ending thirty-three years of consensus adoption (press release GA/12742; the verbatim record was not yet in ODS on 2026-10-09); the natural-disasters text that day (80/113) was adopted by recorded vote; and **ECOSOC adopted no humanitarian resolution in 2020 or 2025** and none is listed for 2026 (official records E/2020/99, E/2021/99, E/2025/99). **79/140 of 9 December 2024 is therefore the Assembly's latest text of the mandate.** Where anticipatory action entered the omnibus:

| Symbol | Adopted (plenary date) | Anticipatory-action language (operative ¶) |
|---|---|---|
| 73/139 | 14 Dec 2018 | ¶36 first appearance: encourages "exploring, developing and, where appropriate, the strengthening of **innovative and anticipatory mechanisms and approaches, such as forecast-based financing and disaster risk insurance**" to reduce the impact of disasters |
| 74/118 | 16 Dec 2019 | ¶36 carried forward unchanged |
| 75/127 | 11 Dec 2020 | ¶36 carried forward unchanged |
| 76/124 | 10 Dec 2021 | ¶37 as above, **plus ¶38**: encourages States, the UN and humanitarian organisations "to continue to **scale up anticipatory approaches, early warning early action systems, forecasting**, prevention-oriented responses and emergency preparedness, and improve **predictive and risk data analytics** across sectors, reinforce systematic risk monitoring, early warning and preparedness capacities at local, national, regional and global levels" |
| 77/28 | 6 Dec 2022 | both paragraphs carried forward (¶38–39) |
| 78/119 | 8 Dec 2023 | both paragraphs carried forward (¶40–41) |
| **79/140** | **9 Dec 2024** | ¶40–41 carried forward; **new ¶59** recalls the Pact for the Future commitments to scale up "timely and predictable funding and **innovative and anticipatory financing mechanisms**" |
| A/80/L.25 | draft of 3 Dec 2025 — **withdrawn 10 Dec 2025, not adopted** | draft ¶42–43 and ¶61 carried all three forward; kept as an extract to show what had been negotiated |

The Assembly's first "anticipatory" sentence is older and sits in the **disaster** resolution — 69/243 OP44 (2014): "shift towards an anticipatory approach to humanitarian crises" — and ECOSOC had "forecast-based financing mechanisms" in 2017/14 OP6 a year before 73/139 (table on [resolutions.md §4](resolutions.md#4-where-the-teams-themes-entered-the-text)). Read together with 46/182 ¶8, ¶19–20 and ¶35(b),(g), this is the General Assembly's basis for OCHA's early-warning, risk-analysis and anticipatory-action work. **The GA text speaks of approaches and mechanisms; it does not define triggers, frameworks, or CERF's anticipatory action window** — those are secretariat practice, documented in [`frameworks/`](../frameworks/) and on [cerf.md](cerf.md) §4. Two further layers complete the picture: Security Council and other Assembly resolutions that task the ERC directly, on [directly-addressed.md](directly-addressed.md), and the yearly budget fascicle in which OCHA itself lists its legislative mandates and plans its work, on [budget.md](budget.md).

## 6. What this means for the team's work

- **Early warning is in the founding text, not an add-on.** The ERC's overview "through the systematic pooling and analysis of early-warning information" (¶35(b)) and the duty to provide "consolidated information, including early warning … particularly [to] affected and disaster-prone countries" (¶35(g); ¶19–20) are the mandate-level hook for monitoring systems such as those in [`pipelines/`](../pipelines/) and for sharing data openly ("in an unrestricted and timely manner", ¶20).
- **Prevention and preparedness were deliberately moved out of OCHA in 1997** (A/51/950 ¶187: to UNDP, as national capacity-building). Anticipatory action re-entered through the *financing* door (60/124's "early action", the forecast-based-financing paragraphs since 73/139), which is why it is framed as acting ahead of a shock within a response mandate, not as disaster-risk reduction.
- **The affected State has the primary role, and assistance is in principle at its request (¶3–4).** Framework design and activation communications should assume government involvement by default; see the framework pages for how each country's framework handles this.
- **Principles are four, and independence is a 2003 addition** — useful when a partner's text cites only three.
