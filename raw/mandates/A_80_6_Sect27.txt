                Overall orientation
                Mandates and background

       27.I.1   The mandate is derived from the priorities established in relevant General Assembly resolutions and
                decisions, including resolution 46/182, in which the Assembly set out the guiding principles of
                humanitarian response and affirmed the leadership role of the Secretary-General to ensure better
                preparation and effective response to natural disasters and complex emergencies. This mandate has
                been confirmed and consolidated through the normative developments of various resolutions over
                the past 33 years. The Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs is responsible for ensuring
                the timely, coherent, coordinated and principled response of the international community to natural
                disasters and complex emergencies and for facilitating the transition from emergency relief to
                rehabilitation and sustainable development. With over 305 million people in need of humanitarian
                assistance in 2025 and extreme poverty and hunger on the rise, owing to the compounded effects of
                emerging and prolonged armed conflicts and the crisis of climate change, ensuring well -coordinated
                and principled humanitarian assistance by the international community remains as imp ortant as ever.


                Strategy and external factors for 2026

       27.I.2   The year 2024 was one of the most challenging in recent history for civilians caught in conflicts. By
                mid-2024, nearly 123 million people had been forcibly displaced by conflict and violence, marking
                the twelfth consecutive annual increase. Armed conflicts continued to take a heavy toll on civilians,
                and the crisis of climate change exacerbated humanitarian needs. The global food security crisis
                affected over 280 million people daily as acute hunger spread and intensified.
       27.I.3   In the face of these challenges, the Office will continue to enhance the coordination of effective and
                efficient delivery of humanitarian assistance, together with humanitarian organizations. The Office
                will continue to develop and promote a common policy on humanitarian issues for the United
                Nations system and its partners; coordinate response to humanitarian emergencies; mobilize United
                Nations capacity to expedite the provision of humanitarian assistance; and strengthen anticipatory
                approaches and early warning early action systems, with special attention paid to those in the most
                vulnerable situations. In addition, the Office will advocate and contribute to better preparedness for
                more timely humanitarian assistance in emergencies; advocate on humanitarian issues; and
                strengthen the analysis and availability of timely information on emergencies and natural disasters,
                in accordance with its mandate. The Office will also support the mobilization of resources for
                countries and communities facing humanitarian emergencies, including host communities, by scaling
                up timely and predictable funding, advancing innovative and anticipatory financing mechanisms and
                strengthening partnerships with international financial institutions to prevent, reduce and respo nd to
                humanitarian suffering. Furthermore, the Office will continue to advocate for safe and unhindered
                humanitarian access, the safety and security of humanitarian personnel, and respect for international
                humanitarian law.
       27.I.4   With regard to cooperation with other entities at the global, regional, national and local levels, the
                Office will continue to work with national and local governments, affected communities and other
                stakeholders, including regional disaster management and response organizations, other regional and
                subregional organizations, private sector actors and first responders, to promote principled, timely
                and effective humanitarian responses.

        27.I.5   With regard to inter-agency coordination and liaison, the Office will continue, through the
                 Inter-Agency Standing Committee and its subsidiary bodies, to coordinate the development of
                 guidance for the humanitarian system, including on operations policy development and advocacy
                 and supporting implementation of the Secretary-General’s Action Agenda on Internal Displacement.
                 The Office will provide and manage global platforms, such as the United Nations Disaster
                 Assessment and Coordination Team mechanism and the International Search and Rescue Advisory
                 Group, to pool resources and facilitate a coordinated response to humanitarian crises. The Office
                 will also collaborate closely with United Nations agencies, funds and programmes, at both the
                 Headquarters and country levels throughout the humanitarian programme cycle, to streamline the
                 current guidance and indicators associated with thematic humanitarian issues. This will enable
                 effective and consistent system-wide response strategies that meet the humanitarian needs of people
                 in vulnerable situations and minimize duplication and gaps in response efforts. The Office’s country,
                 regional and liaison offices are strategically located to work with governments and assist in
                 humanitarian preparedness and response.
        27.I.6   With regard to the external factors, the overall plan for 2026 is based on the following planning
                 assumptions:
                 (a)   Natural disasters will continue to increase in frequency and magnitude, owing, among other
                       things, to the adverse effects of the crisis of climate change;
                 (b)   The impact of the global economic contraction, compounded by the impacts of natural disasters
                       and armed conflicts, will continue to be felt and will increase the vulnerability of people;
                 (c)   Humanitarian needs and funding requirements will remain high.
        27.I.7   The Office integrates a gender perspective into its operational activities, deliverables and results, as
                 appropriate, including through gender analysis, prevention, response and mitigation of gender-based
                 violence, and by promoting the participation and leadership of women in decision-making.
        27.I.8   The Office advances United Nations system-wide commitments on protection from sexual
                 exploitation and abuse and sexual harassment. The Under-Secretary-General for Humanitarian
                 Affairs and Emergency Relief Coordinator, as Chair of the Inter-Agency Standing Committee, leads
                 and promotes Committee efforts that are aimed at ensuring that people caught up in crises feel safe
                 and respected and can gain access to the protection and assistance that they need. The Office will
                 promote system-wide commitments to ensure that the affected population and personnel engaged in
                 the delivery of humanitarian assistance are protected from sexual harassment and abuse.
        27.I.9   In line with the United Nations Disability Inclusion Strategy, the Office will work to make
                 humanitarian programming and responses more inclusive of persons with disabilities, in particular
                 by ensuring that their needs are reflected in humanitarian data. On the basis of gaps identified
                 through the previous implementation of the Strategy, the Office will develop an action plan outlining
                 the institutional and operational changes necessary to ensure disability inclusion.

                   Policy and analysis

                   Objective

      27.I.15      The objective, to which this subprogramme contributes, is to improve the strategic and operational
                   coherence of humanitarian response.


                   Strategy

      27.I.16      To contribute to the objective, the subprogramme will:
                   (a)     Identify emerging humanitarian trends to support the comprehensive, authoritative and
                           evidence-based assessment of global humanitarian needs and analyse challenges and
                           opportunities for humanitarian policy and programme development;
                   (b)     Identify policy and programmatic solutions, best practices and lessons learned to be applied in
                           the humanitarian field and take steps to increase preparedness and operational readiness to
                           respond to humanitarian priorities and reduce humanitarian needs.

                       humanitarian needs and response capacity;

                 (c)   Humanitarian actors applying innovative solutions in humanitarian operations.


                 Programme performance in 2024

                 Humanitarian practitioners identified pathways for strengthening humanitarian assistance
       27.I.18   In December 2024, the subprogramme, in collaboration with the United Nations Foundation,
                 convened the thirteenth annual Global Humanitarian Policy Forum, bringing together leaders and
                 practitioners to reflect on the state of the global humanitarian landscape. Through a series of virtual
                 interactive sessions, participants discussed the most pressing challenges facing humanitarian
                 assistance efforts and explored new pathways to enhance the system’s ability to adapt to change and
                 respond. The theme of the 2024 Policy Forum underscored the urgent need to rethink and reshape
                 humanitarian strategies in response to increasingly complex challenges and escalating levels of
                 humanitarian need. It concluded with the development of actionable recommendations for
                 humanitarian agencies, donors and partners, including to further promote community-driven
                 approaches to humanitarian action, scale up climate action, depoliticize humanitarian assistance and
                 foster strategic, inclusive leadership.

                   Subprogramme 2
                   Coordination of humanitarian action and emergency response

                   Objective

      27.I.28      The objective, to which this subprogramme contributes, is to ensure a coherent, effective and timely
                   humanitarian response to reduce and alleviate human suffering in natural disasters and complex
                   emergencies.


                   Strategy

      27.I.29      To contribute to the objective, the subprogramme will:
                   (a)     Maintain an effective coordination system to convene humanitarian actors and support senior
                           humanitarian leadership at the global level and in countries affected by natural disasters and
                           complex emergencies, by coordinating timely and objective multisectoral assessments and
                           analyses of humanitarian needs; supporting response planning; and programming and
                           monitoring the provision of assistance to people in need during and in the aftermath of
                           emergencies;
                   (b)     Promote the delivery of principled humanitarian assistance and coordinate resource
                           mobilization efforts to reduce funding gaps that hamper humanitarian operations, including
                           through effective partnerships with humanitarian actors, Member States, regi onal organizations
                           and civil society, and the effective management and disbursement of funding from
                           humanitarian pooled funds;
                   (c)     Increase the use of anticipatory approaches and early warning early action systems to prevent
                           and reduce human suffering, especially with regard to disasters relating to the crisis of climate
                           change;

25-04935                                                                                                                 11/52
Part VI           Human rights and humanitarian affairs

                  (d)     Strengthen links between humanitarian assistance and efforts to support resilience, early
                          recovery and development to reduce the needs and vulnerabilities of affected people, ensuring
                          that sex-, age- and disability-oriented data are taken into account in all aspects of the response.
        27.I.30   The above-mentioned work is expected to result in:
                  (a)     Improved prioritization of humanitarian responses, based on informed and timely decision-
                          making by humanitarian actors;
                  (b)     Reduced duplication of response interventions and reduced gaps in humanitarian response;
                  (c)     An adequately funded international humanitarian system, to respond to increasingly frequent
                          natural disasters and complex emergencies.


                  Programme performance in 2024

                  Reaching people in need in increasingly challenging contexts

                   Result 3: scaled up anticipatory approaches and early warning early action systems
                   Proposed programme plan for 2026
        27.I.37    Anticipatory approaches and early warning early action systems are focused on action before
                   predictable disasters strike, with a view to preventing and reducing human suffering and assisting
                   those in need. In its resolution 79/140, the General Assembly encouraged the United Nations and
                   humanitarian organizations to continue to scale up anticipatory approaches and early warning early
                   action systems and encouraged exploring, developing and, where appropriate, strengthening
                   innovative and anticipatory mechanisms and approaches, such as forecast-based financing and
                   disaster risk insurance, to reduce the impact of disasters and address humanitarian needs.

                   Lessons learned and planned change
        27.I.38    The lesson for the subprogramme, based on pilot projects, was that anticipatory approaches and early
                   warning early action systems are effective in preventing and reducing human suffering and economic
                   losses. The subprogramme is using predictive data to take action before disasters strike, through the
                   establishment of pre-agreed anticipatory action frameworks under which predefined humanitarian
                   interventions are triggered when specific forecasts indicate a high likelihood of a shock. This allows
                   for a faster and more effective response by releasing pre-arranged funding, with a view to mitigating
                   the impact before the crisis fully unfolds. Further scaling up of anticipatory approaches and early
                   warning early action systems is needed, including through the consistent use of anticipatory
                   financing mechanisms to ensure timely and predictable funding. In applying the lesson, the
                   subprogramme will scale up anticipatory approaches and early warning early action systems to cover
                   more countries and hazards, including through increased financing by humanitarian pooled funds for
                   the coordination of such approaches.

           II. United Nations Office for Disaster Risk Reduction
             A. Proposed programme plan for 2026 and programme performance
                in 2024
                Overall orientation
                Mandates and background

      27.II.1   The United Nations Office for Disaster Risk Reduction is the focal point for the coordination of
                disaster reduction, ensuring synergies among the disaster reduction activities of the United Nations
                system and regional organizations and activities in socioeconomic and humanitarian fields. It
                provides a data-driven and people-centred approach to supporting Governments and stakeholders in
                identifying, reporting on and reducing existing and emerging disaster risks, with the aim of ensuring
                that no one is left behind in implementing the Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction 2015 –
                2030. The mandate derives from the priorities established in General Assembly resolutions and
                decisions, including resolutions 42/169, 54/219, 56/195 and 77/164, as well as the Sendai
                Declaration and the Sendai Framework adopted by the Assembly in its resolution 69/283.
      27.II.2   The General Assembly, in its resolution 69/283, tasked the Office with supporting the
                implementation, follow-up and review of the Sendai Framework. The Assembly, in its resolution
                77/289, adopted the political declaration of the high-level meeting on the midterm review of the
                Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction 2015–2030, which reaffirmed the importance of
                effective implementation of the Sendai Framework. The Office’s programme plan aligns with the
                outcomes of the midterm review of the implementation of the Sendai Framework. To implement its
                mandate, in line with its strategic framework, the Office supports regional, national and local

                civil society, will support an all-of-society approach to disaster risk reduction. The Office will

                   Proposed programme plan for 2026
     27.II.18      The General Assembly, in its resolution 77/289, adopted the political declaration of the high-level
                   meeting on the midterm review of the Sendai Framework, in which Heads of State and Government,
                   Ministers and High Representatives expressed deep concern that public and private investments to
                   anticipate, plan for, reduce and prevent disaster risk remained insufficient and did not match the
                   scale of existing and future risk. In the same resolution, the Assembly called upon States to invest
                   in disaster risk reduction, including by developing comprehensive national and local disaster risk
                   reduction financing strategies that leveraged the full spectrum of pre- and post-disaster financial
                   resources from public, private, domestic and international sources.

                   Lessons learned and planned change
     27.II.19      The lesson for the programme was the importance of supporting Member States in developing more
                   comprehensive financing strategies for disaster risk reduction. In disaster-related financing
                   strategies, disproportionate emphasis is often placed on post-disaster financing needs and
                   instruments, while the financing of preventive measures is sometimes overlooked. In applying the
                   lesson, the programme will support activities aimed at ensuring the integration of disaster risk
                   reduction into future financing strategies by assisting countries in identifying policy reforms,
                   institutional arrangements and approaches that serve to unlock financing for risk reduction and
                   complement post-disaster financing instruments.
